# Lecture 16: Applied Case of Discrete Probability Distribution in Quality Control

Overview
This lecture presents a practical application of binomial probability distribution in managerial decision- making through a quality control case study for a breakfast cereal company. The session demonstrates how discrete probability concepts are used to establish production standards and make critical business decisions.
Case Background: Go Bananas Cereal Product
Company and Product:
- Company: GIG (Go Great Indian Grains Ltd.) - a breakfast cereal manufacturer
- New Product: "Go Bananas" - a 16-ounce box containing rice flakes and banana-flavored marshmallows
- Target Specification: Banana-flavored marshmallows must weigh between 50 grams and 75 grams per box
Key Parameters:
- Random sample size: 25 boxes per week
- Acceptable weight range: 50-75 grams
- Current process failure rate: 8% (boxes outside specification)
- Shutdown criteria: If 5 or more boxes fail to meet weight standards, production stops for inspection
Problem 1: Probability of Production Shutdown Under Normal Operations
Objective: Determine the probability that a weekly sample will trigger production shutdown even when the process is functioning properly.
Approach:
- Use binomial probability distribution (two outcomes: pass or fail)
- Parameters: n = 25 (sample size), p = 0.08 (failure probability)
- Question: What is P(X ≥ 5)?
Solution Using Excel:
- Applied BINOM.DIST function with cumulative = FALSE to calculate individual probabilities
- Calculated probabilities for 0 to 25 failures
- Summed probabilities from 5 to 25 failures
- Alternative method: 1 - P(X ≤ 4)
- Result: 4.5% probability that production will shutdown during normal operations
Key Excel Technique:
- Use absolute cell references ($F$1) to keep sample size and probability values constant when copying formulas
- Distinguish between probability mass function (FALSE) and cumulative probability (TRUE)
Problem 2: Adjusting Shutdown Criteria to Reduce False Alarms
Objective: Reduce the shutdown probability from 4.5% to no more than 1% while maintaining the same sample size and failure rate.
Analysis of Different Threshold Levels:
- If threshold = 4 failures: 13.5% shutdown probability (too high)
- If threshold = 5 failures: 4.5% shutdown probability (current)
- If threshold = 6 failures: 1.5% shutdown probability (still above 1%)
- If threshold = 7 failures: 0.2-0.3% shutdown probability (meets goal)
Decision: Change the shutdown criteria from 5 boxes to 7 boxes to achieve less than 1% false alarm rate Caveat: Allowing 7 out of 25 boxes (28%) to fail may be too lenient and harm product quality reputation
Problem 3: Improving the Production Process to Maintain Strict Standards
Objective: Keep the shutdown threshold at 5 boxes while reducing the false alarm rate to 1% or less by improving the production process.
Approach:
- Reduce the process failure rate from 8% by implementing process improvements
- Calculate required failure rates using different p values
Process Improvement Analysis:
- At p = 0.08 (8% failure): 4.5% shutdown probability
- At p = 0.07 (7% failure): 2.7% shutdown probability
- At p = 0.06 (6% failure): 1.5% shutdown probability
- At p = 0.05 (5% failure): ~1% shutdown probability (achieves goal)
Solution: Reduce the process failure rate from 8% to approximately 5% through process improvements such as:
- Machine maintenance and calibration
- Packaging system adjustments
- Quality control enhancements
Key Business Insights
Three Possible Approaches to Meet Quality Goals:
- Relax inspection criteria: Increase acceptable failures from 5 to 7 boxes (not recommended - damages brand quality)
- Improve process capability: Reduce failure rate from 8% to 5% (recommended - strengthens operations)
- Increase sample size: Test more boxes per week (increases inspection costs)
Important Concept: A 100% error-free process is statistically impossible. Management must balance quality goals with operational feasibility by accepting some baseline failure rate while implementing continuous process improvements.
Excel Techniques Demonstrated
- BINOM.DIST function: Calculates binomial probabilities with parameters (number_s, trials, probability_s, cumulative)
- Absolute references: Use $ signs to lock cells when copying formulas
- Paste Special: Use "Paste as Numbers" to copy values without formulas
- Cumulative vs. Mass Functions:
  - FALSE = exact probability of specific outcome
  - TRUE = cumulative probability up to that outcome
