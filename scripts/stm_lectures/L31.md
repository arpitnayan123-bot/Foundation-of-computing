# Lecture 31: Two-Tail Hypothesis Testing and Population Proportion Testing

Overview
This lecture covers advanced hypothesis testing techniques, including two-tail tests, tests with unknown population standard deviation, and tests about population proportions. The P-value approach and critical value approach are applied to solve various real-world problems.
Two-Tail Hypothesis Testing
Concept and Setup
- A two-tail hypothesis test occurs when the null hypothesis states that the population mean equals a specific value (H₀: μ = μ₀) and the alternative hypothesis states it does not equal that value (Hₐ: μ ≠ μ₀)
- The rejection region exists in both tails of the distribution
P-Value Approach for Two-Tail Tests
- Calculate the test statistic (Z)
- Determine if the test statistic falls in the upper or lower tail
- Calculate the probability for one tail only
- Double the obtained area to account for both tails due to symmetry
- Reject H₀ if P-value ≤ α
Critical Value Approach for Two-Tail Tests
- Critical values occur at both the lower tail (-Z_{α/2}) and upper tail (+Z_{α/2})
- Reject H₀ if the test statistic is less than -Z_{α/2} or greater than Z_{α/2}
- The rejection regions are symmetrical on both sides of the distribution
Case Study: Toothpaste Filling Process
Problem Context
- Production line designed to fill toothpaste tubes with mean weight of 6 oz
- Quality assurance periodically tests 30 tubes to verify the filling process
- Both underfilling and overfilling are undesirable
Given Data
- Sample mean: 6.1 oz
- Sample standard deviation: 0.2 oz
- Sample size: 30 tubes
- Significance level: α = 0.03
Hypotheses
- H₀: μ = 6.0 oz
- Hₐ: μ ≠ 6.0 oz
Solution
- Test statistic: Z = 2.74
- P-value = 2 × (1 - 0.9969) = 0.0062
- Since P-value (0.0062) < α (0.03), reject H₀
- Conclusion: There is sufficient statistical evidence that the mean filling weight is not 6 oz; the process requires adjustment
Critical Value Approach
- Z_{α/2} = ±2.17
- Since Z = 2.74 > 2.17, reject H₀
Confidence Interval Approach to Two-Tail Tests
Method
- Develop a confidence interval for the population mean using the sample data
- If the hypothesized value (μ₀) falls within the confidence interval, do not reject H₀
- If the hypothesized value falls outside the interval, reject H₀
Toothpaste Example
- 97% confidence interval: [6.02, 6.17]
- Hypothesized mean: 6.0 oz
- Since 6.0 is outside the interval, reject H₀
Hypothesis Testing with Unknown Population Standard Deviation
Key Differences
- Use t-distribution instead of standard normal distribution
- Test statistic: t = (x̄ - μ)/(s/√n) with (n-1) degrees of freedom
- Rejection rules remain the same as with known standard deviation
Case Study: Highway Speed Testing
Problem Context
- State highway patrol samples vehicle speeds at various locations
- H₀: μ ≤ 65 mph (one-tail test)
- Hₐ: μ > 65 mph
- Significance level: α = 0.05
Given Data
- Sample size: 64 vehicles
- Sample mean: 66.2 mph
- Sample standard deviation: 4.2 mph
Solution
- Test statistic: t = 2.286 with 63 degrees of freedom
- P-value: between 0.01 and 0.05
- Since P-value < α (0.05), reject H₀
- Conclusion: At least 95% confident that mean speed exceeds 65 mph; location is suitable for radar trap
Critical Value Approach
- Critical value: t_{0.05} = 1.669
- Since t = 2.286 > 1.669, reject H₀
Hypothesis Testing About Population Proportion
Setup and Assumptions
Three possible forms of hypotheses:
- H₀: p = p₀ vs Hₐ: p > p₀ (right-tail)
- H₀: p = p₀ vs Hₐ: p < p₀ (left-tail)
- H₀: p = p₀ vs Hₐ: p ≠ p₀ (two-tail)
The equality always appears in the null hypothesis
Assumptions for Normal Distribution
- np₀ > 5
- n(1 - p₀) > 5
Test Statistic
Z = (p̂ - p₀)/√[p₀(1-p₀)/n], where p̂ is the sample proportion
Case Study: Drunk Driving Accidents
Problem Context
- National Safety Council claims 50% of accidents during Christmas/New Year are caused by drunk driving
- Sample data: 120 accidents with 67 caused by drunk driving
- Significance level: α = 0.05
Hypotheses
- H₀: p = 0.5
- Hₐ: p ≠ 0.5 (two-tail test)
Solution
- Sample proportion: p̂ = 67/120 = 0.558
- Standard error: σ_{p̂} = 0.045
- Test statistic: Z = 1.278
- P-value = 2 × (1 - 0.8997) = 0.20
- Since P-value (0.20) > α (0.05), do not reject H₀
- Conclusion: Insufficient evidence that the true proportion of drunk driving accidents differs from 50%
Critical Value Approach
- Critical values: Z_{0.025} = ±1.96
- Since 1.278 falls between -1.96 and 1.96, do not reject H₀
