# Lecture 33: Exam Preparation and Course Overview

This session is exclusively dedicated to addressing student doubts regarding quizzes and the final exam, with no new content covered.
Exam Structure and Format
- Duration: 2 hours
- Total Marks: 60 marks
- Question Types: Mixed set including very short questions, medium-length questions, and questions with multiple subparts
- Weightage: End-term exam (60 marks) + Quizzes (40% weightage)
- Syllabus: Chapters 1-9 from Anderson and Sweeney textbook, with emphasis on Chapters 1-8
- Key Focus: Largely based on practice questions provided and content discussed in recorded sessions
Essential Materials and Resources
- Calculator: Scientific (non-programmable) calculator is mandatory; normal calculators are not sufficient for calculations
- Provided Materials: Z-tables and other essential data/information will be provided in the question paper
- No Need to Carry: Statistical tables, normal tables, or reference materials - all required values will be given
- Personal Items: Students must bring their own pen, calculator, and other necessary materials
- Question Paper and Answer Sheet: Only these will be provided by exam centers
Study Recommendations
- Primary Resources: Practice questions and recorded session discussions are 100% sufficient to answer all exam questions
- Optional References: Textbook or LLMs (Large Language Models) for deeper understanding if needed
- Sample Paper: Sample question paper will be provided by end of week showing exact exam pattern and question distribution
- Practice Strategy: Complete watching recorded sessions before solving related practice questions to improve clarity
- Emphasis on Numericals: Statistics requires hands-on practice; conceptual understanding develops through solving numerical problems rather than theory alone
Key Concepts: Population vs. Sample
- Population: Set of all possible values relevant to the research question (e.g., all 1.4 billion people in India for gender ratio analysis)
- Sample: Any subset less than the entire population
- Formula Application: Use "n-1" (sample formula) for variance when dealing with sample data; use "n" for population data
- Recognition Method: Apply n vs. n-1 rule to determine if data is population or sample
Answer Guidelines
- Answer Length: No specific word limit required; answers should be concise and numerical rather than wordy
- Attempt All Questions: Students should attempt every question even if unsure about final answers
- Explanation Approach: Write answers as if explaining to someone unfamiliar with statistics, showing all essential steps
- Question Sequence: Answers can be solved in any sequence as long as appropriate question numbers are clearly marked
- Conceptual Questions: Few conceptual questions may appear but are derived directly from numerical problems; solving numericals ensures ability to answer conceptual questions
Study Timeline and Support
- Recorded Sessions: Most students have not completed watching videos; completion is strongly recommended
- Quiz Pending: One remaining quiz scheduled for next week
- Office Hours: Instructor will remain available online for another 10-20 minutes to address doubts
- Post-Session Support: Students can post questions on LMS for clarification
