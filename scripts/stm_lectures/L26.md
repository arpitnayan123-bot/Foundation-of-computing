# Lecture 26: Understanding Transmission Failure Through Descriptive Statistics

This lecture presents a real-world case study where Metropolitan Research, a consumer research organization, investigates transmission failures in automobiles produced by a major manufacturer through statistical analysis.
Case Overview
- Organization: Metropolitan Research conducts consumer satisfaction surveys on various products and services
- Focus: Evaluating customer satisfaction with automobile transmission performance
- Problem Identified: Questionnaire responses from car owners revealed multiple complaints about early transmission failures
- Data Source: Sample of 50 actual transmission repair records from a transmission repair firm in Detroit
- Data Type: Number of kilometers driven before transmission failure for each vehicle
Descriptive Statistics Analysis
Key Statistical Measures Calculated:
- Mean: 73,340 miles (average distance before transmission failure)
- Median: 72,700 miles (50% of vehicles fail below this point, 50% above)
- Standard Deviation: 24,849 miles (indicates high variability in failure points)
- Mode: None (no two vehicles failed at exactly the same mileage, showing no pattern concentration)
- Skewness: Slightly positive and close to zero (data is relatively symmetrical, not heavily skewed)
Interpretation of Findings:
- Of 50 vehicles sampled, 25 failed before 72,700 miles and 25 failed after
- The high standard deviation indicates substantial variation in when transmission failures occur
- The absence of mode suggests failures are distributed across a wide range rather than clustering at specific mileage points
Excel Tools for Statistical Analysis
How to Access Descriptive Statistics:
- Navigate to Data tab → Data Analysis tool pack
- If not available, go to File → Options → Add-ins → Analysis Tool Pack
- Select Descriptive Statistics and configure:
  - Input range (select your data column)
  - Enable "Labels in First Row" if applicable
  - Request confidence interval (95%)
  - Output to new worksheet for better visualization
Visualizing Data Distribution:
- Create histograms to display failure frequency across mileage ranges
- Use conditional formatting to identify duplicate values
- Visual representation shows more failures occurring at lower mileage ranges
Confidence Interval Estimation
95% Confidence Interval for Population Mean:
- Calculation: Mean ± Margin of Error = 73,340 ± 7,076
- Confidence Interval: 66,264 to 80,416 miles
- Interpretation: We are 95% confident that the true population mean number of miles before transmission failure falls between 66,264 and 80,416 miles
Critical Observation:
- 19 vehicles failed below 66,264 miles (earlier than confidence interval)
- 18 vehicles failed above 80,416 miles (later than confidence interval)
- Early failures below the lower confidence bound suggest potential serious quality issues
Sample Size Calculation
Objective: Determine sample size needed to estimate population mean within a margin of error of 5,000 miles at 95% confidence level Formula Used: n = (Z_(α/2))² × σ² / E² Where:
- Z_(α/2) = 1.96 (for 95% confidence level)
- σ = 24,849 miles (planned standard deviation from previous sample)
- E = 5,000 miles (desired margin of error)
Calculation: n = (1.96)² × (24,849)² / (5,000)² ≈ 95.26
Recommendation: Increase sample size to approximately 100 vehicles to reduce margin of error and improve estimate accuracy
Additional Information Needed for Comprehensive Analysis
Supply-Side Factors to Investigate:
- Defects in specific production batches
- Material quality issues
- Problems with particular manufacturing plants or machinery
- Supplier quality variations
Demand-Side Factors to Investigate:
- Driver behavior and driving techniques
- Road conditions and terrain (off-road usage, highway vs. city driving)
- Geographic location and climate conditions (rainy seasons, wet conditions)
- Vehicle maintenance patterns and practices
- Environmental and infrastructure factors
Managerial Implications and Next Steps
Key Takeaway for Managers:
- While average failure occurs around 73,000 miles, a significant portion of vehicles fail much earlier
- Early failures represent a critical quality and warranty concern
- Statistical averages must be considered alongside the distribution of outliers
Path Forward:
- Expand data collection to include multiple variables (production batch, driver information, road conditions, climate data)
- Employ advanced statistical techniques such as regression analysis or machine learning models
- Correlate transmission failures with supply-chain and customer-use variables to identify root causes
- Use multi-variable analysis to determine which factors most significantly influence transmission durability
This case demonstrates how descriptive statistics provide essential insights for managerial decision- making, while also highlighting the limitations of univariate analysis and the need for more sophisticated analytical approaches when comprehensive problem evaluation is required.
