# Lecture 15: Application of Normal Probability Distribution in Inventory Management

Overview
This lecture discusses the Specialty Toys case, which demonstrates how normal probability distribution can be applied to real-world business scenarios, specifically inventory management and order quantity decisions for new product launches.
The Business Problem
Specialty Toys' Challenge:
- Specialty Toys launches new innovative toys during the pre-holiday season
- They must place one-time orders with manufacturers in June or July for October delivery
- The company faces a critical decision: determining optimal order quantity for new products
- The Core Dilemma: Ordering too few units results in lost sales; ordering too many leaves unsold inventory that must be sold at deep discounts
The Weather Teddy Product:
- A new talking teddy bear with a built-in barometer that predicts weather
- Five different weather-related responses when a child presses the bear's hand
- Predictions have been surprisingly accurate in testing
Key Financial Data
Pricing Structure:
- Regular selling price: $24 per unit
- Cost per unit: $16
- Profit per unit (in-season): $8
- Clearance/salvage price: $5 per unit
- Loss per unit (off-season): $11
Demand Information:
- Expected demand: 20,000 units
- 95% probability that demand falls between 10,000 to 30,000 units
- Management suggestions for order quantities: 15,000, 18,000, 24,000, or 28,000 units
Statistical Analysis Using Normal Distribution
Establishing the Normal Curve
- Mean (μ) = 20,000 units (expected demand)
- Since demand is normally distributed and symmetric, 2.5% probability exists on each tail
- Use Z-score of 1.96 for the 97.5 percentile
Calculating Standard Deviation
Formula: Z = (X - μ) / σ
1.96 = (30,000 - 20,000) / σ
Standard deviation (σ) = 5,102 units
Probability of Stockout Analysis
Stockout Definition: Occurs when demand exceeds the order quantity Calculating Stockout Probability:
- Use NORM.DIST function with formula: Z = (Order Quantity - Mean) / Standard Deviation
- Stockout probability = 1 - NORM.DIST result
Results for Management Suggestions:
- Order 15,000 units: ~16.35% probability of stockout
- Order 18,000 units: Lower stockout probability
- Order 24,000 units: Even lower stockout probability
- Order 28,000 units: Minimal stockout probability
Profit Scenarios Under Different Demand Levels
Three Demand Scenarios Analyzed: 10,000 units (worst case), 20,000 units (most likely), 30,000 units (best case)
Order Quantity: 15,000 units
- Demand 10,000: Profit = $25,000
- Demand 20,000: Profit = $120,000
- Demand 30,000: Profit = $120,000 (capped by stock)
Order Quantity: 18,000 units
- Demand 10,000: Loss = $8,000
- Demand 20,000: Profit = $168,000
- Demand 30,000: Profit = $168,000
Order Quantity: 24,000 units
- Demand 10,000: Loss = $74,000
- Demand 20,000: Profit = $116,000
- Demand 30,000: Profit = $192,000
Order Quantity: 28,000 units
- Demand 10,000: Loss = $100,000
- Demand 20,000: Profit = $72,000
- Demand 30,000: Profit = $200,000
Risk-Based Order Quantity Determination
Scenario: 70% Service Level (30% Stockout Tolerance)
- Use NORM.S.INV(0.70) to find Z-score = 0.52
- Order quantity formula: Q = (Z × σ) + μ
- Q = (0.52 × 5,102) + 20,000 = 22,653 units
Profit Results at 22,653 units:
- Demand 10,000: Loss = $59,083
- Demand 20,000: Profit = $130,000
- Demand 30,000: Profit = $181,000
Key Insights
Trade-offs in Order Quantity
- Higher order quantities maximize profit potential but increase risk of losses in low-demand scenarios
- Lower order quantities reduce downside risk but limit profit when demand is high
- Optimal decision depends on management's risk tolerance
Statistical Advantage
- Using statistical analysis provides better decision-making than random guessing
- Probability distributions allow managers to quantify risks and expected outcomes
- Enables informed choices aligned with business risk appetite
Management Consideration
- Higher profit potential may justify accepting greater risk of stockout
- Different managers can make different decisions based on their risk preferences
- Statistical framework supports multiple valid strategic approaches
