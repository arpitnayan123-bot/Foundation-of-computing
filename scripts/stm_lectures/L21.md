# Lecture 21: Marrian Dairies Yogurt Market Entry Case Study

Overview
This lecture presents a practical application of sampling and sampling distributions through a real-world business scenario involving Marrian Dairies' decision to launch a second yogurt flavor. The company uses statistical analysis to determine market expansion opportunities while avoiding cannibalization of existing product sales.
Background and Business Context
Initial Product Success:
- Marrian Dairies successfully launched BlueGurt (blueberry flavored yogurt)
- Consumer ratings: Mean = 80, Standard Deviation = 25 on a 100-point scale
- Purchase threshold: Customers with ratings > 75 are likely to purchase
New Product Decision:
- Marketing department proposes launching StrawGurt (strawberry flavored yogurt)
- Primary goal: Expand market share by appealing to customers who dislike BlueGurt
- Concern: Avoid cannibalization (cannibalizing existing sales of BlueGurt)
Statistical Problem Setup
Given Information:
- Population mean (μ) = 80
- Population standard deviation (σ) = 25
- Sample size (n) = 50 customers
- Target threshold = 75 or less
Research Question: What is the probability that a random sample of 50 customers will give a mean score of 75 or less for BlueGurt?
Sampling Distribution Analysis
Key Calculations:
- Standard error of the mean: σₓ = σ/√n = 25/√50 = 3.5355
- Z-score calculation: Z = (X - μ)/σₓ = (75 - 80)/3.5355 = -1.414
- Probability: P(X̄ ≤ 75) = P(Z ≤ -1.414) = 0.0786 or 7.86%
This means there is approximately a 7.86% chance that sampled customers will rate BlueGurt at 75 or less, indicating potential customers for StrawGurt.
Impact of Sample Size Increase
Scenario with Sample Size = 150:
- As sample size increases, the standard error decreases
- Probability that mean score ≤ 75 reduces to approximately 0.007 or 0.7%
- Larger samples provide more precise estimates closer to the true population mean
Key Insight: Increasing sample size improves accuracy of estimates, as sample means converge closer to the population mean.
Extended Probability Analysis
Example: Probability of Score Between 75 and 85
- Lower limit (x₁) = 75
- Upper limit (x₂) = 85
- Z₁ = -1.414, Z₂ = 1.414
- Probability = 84.2%
Impact of Parameter Changes:
- Increasing sample size → increases probability (more concentrated distribution)
- Widening range → increases probability (larger area under curve)
- Narrowing range → decreases probability (smaller area under curve)

Business Implications
- A 7.86% probability of customers rating BlueGurt ≤ 75 suggests a relatively small segment of dissatisfied customers
- This limited market suggests caution in launching StrawGurt as it may not significantly expand total market share
- Statistical analysis provides data-driven foundation for product launch decisions
- Larger sample sizes provide more reliable insights for management decision-making
Practical Extensions
The methodology can be applied to:
- Calculate confidence intervals around sample means
- Determine optimal sample sizes for desired precision levels
- Assess probability of various rating ranges
- Support risk assessment in market expansion strategies
