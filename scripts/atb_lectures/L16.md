# Lecture 16: 16: Graph Properties and Relationship Representation in Networks

Lecture 16: Graph Properties and Relationship
Representation in Networks
This lecture explores how relationships and connections are depicted in graphs, focusing on the key
properties that distinguish different types of graphs. The discussion emphasizes practical applications in
social media analysis and network structures.
Directed vs. Undirected Graphs
Directed Graphs
Relationships are defined in a specific direction only
The opposite relationship is not automatically true
Example: "A is a son of B" does not mean "B is a son of A"
Different relationships in opposite directions are considered separate (e.g., "son of" vs. "father of")
Use directed graphs when direction of relationship is important
Undirected Graphs
Relationships have no specific direction
If a relationship exists in one direction, it is true in both directions
Example: "A is a cousin of B" means "B is a cousin of A" (mutual relationship)
Use undirected graphs when interested only in connections without directional importance
Social Media Examples
Facebook friendship: Undirected graph (mutual friendship once request is accepted)
Instagram follows: Directed graph (following is not reciprocal)
LinkedIn connections: Both types (connections are undirected, follows are directed)
In-Degree and Out-Degree
In Degree
Number of edges coming into a vertex
In social media: number of users or pages that follow you
Out Degree
Number of edges going out from a vertex
In social media: number of users or pages that you follow
Practical Application - Instagram Influencer Marketing
In-degree is crucial for influencer selection
Influencers with high in-degree (many followers) guarantee audience for marketing
Out-degree (who they follow) is not relevant for marketing purposes
Marketing firms prioritize users with high in-degree

Weighted vs. Unweighted Graphs
Unweighted Graphs
All connections have equal value
Example: "A knows B" treated the same as any other knowing relationship
No strength or value assigned to connections
Weighted Graphs
Each connection has a strength value based on context
Strength varies depending on relationship type and interaction frequency
Examples:
Family relationships: Siblings have stronger connection than distant cousins
Flight networks: More flights between destinations = higher weight
Social media recommendations: Frequently watched content has higher weight
YouTube Recommendation Example
High weight: Videos from subscribed channels watched frequently result in stronger connections and
more recommendations
Low weight: New, unwatched channels or one-time recommendations have minimal weight
Recommender systems prioritize content with high connection strength
Connected vs. Disconnected Graphs
Connected Graphs
Every vertex can be reached from every other vertex
Paths may vary in length
Example: Within a community, members are connected through direct or indirect relationships
Disconnected Graphs
Certain vertices or subgraphs may be isolated
No connection exists between some sections
Example: Port Blair (island) has no road connection to mainland India
Practical Example
Amdabad to Silchar (road network): Connected graph
Amdabad to Port Blair (road network only): Disconnected graph
Cyclic vs. Acyclic Graphs
Cyclic Graphs
Possibility exists to return to the starting vertex through different paths
Multiple pathways connect vertices, creating loops
Example: A-B-C-A (can return to starting point without retracing steps)
Social example: Friend networks where A is friends with B and C, and B is friends with C
Acyclic Graphs
No path returns to the starting vertex without retracing the same route

Terminal endpoints exist in the network
Example: Parent-child relationships (cannot return to parent if moving forward only)
Social example: Organizational hierarchies or metro networks with terminal stations
Practical Examples
Airport network with return flights: Cyclic graph (flights exist in both directions between cities)
Metro line network: Acyclic graph (terminal stations prevent returning without retracing paths)
Summary of Graph Properties
Graphs can be characterized by multiple independent properties:
Direction: Directed or undirected
Weight: Weighted or unweighted
Connectivity: Connected or disconnected
Cycles: Cyclic or acyclic
These properties work together to represent complex real-world networks and relationships in social
media, transportation systems, and other domains.
