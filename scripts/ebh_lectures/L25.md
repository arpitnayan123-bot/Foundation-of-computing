# Lecture 25: Evolution of Management Thought and Practices: From Taylor to Fayol

Overview
This lecture examines the major figures in the evolution of management thought and practices, building on previous discussions of the division of labor and scientific management. The focus is on Henry Fayol's contributions to management theory and how his framework differs from Frederick Taylor's approach.
While Taylor concentrated on factory floor efficiency, Fayol developed a comprehensive management philosophy applicable to overall business administration.
Frederick Taylor's Scientific Management (Taylorism) - Review
Key Principles:
- Principle 1: Develop a rule of science for each aspect of a job through time and motion studies
- Principle 2: Scientifically select and train workers based on job requirements rather than favoritism or nepotism
- Principle 3: Management and workforce should work together collaboratively
- Principle 4: Divide work and responsibility equally between management and workers
Characteristics:
- Focused exclusively on factory floor efficiency and task optimization
- Applied scientific methods to standardize work processes and worker selection
- Criticized for treating workers as mechanized objects without considering their emotions, thoughts, and personal needs
- Limited worker involvement to execution only, while planning remained with management
Henry Fayol's Contributions to Management Theory (Fayolism)
Background and Perspective
- French engineer and senior executive who served as CEO of a mining firm from the 1880s onward
- Developed theories from a top-level administrative perspective, looking at the entire organization
- Unlike Taylor's bottom-up approach, Fayol provided a top-down view of organizational management
- Worked parallelly with Taylor in different geographies (Europe vs. United States) during the same historical period
Six Industrial and Business Activities
Fayol identified six major activities necessary for running a business or factory:
1. Technical Activity: Production, manufacturing, and adaptation of products
  - Encompasses all operational aspects including factory layout and production processes
  - Taylor's work on shop floor efficiency falls under this category
2. Commercial Activity: Buying, selling, and exchange of goods
  - Includes procurement of raw materials and sales of finished products
  - Encompasses advertising, marketing, and supplier relationships
3. Financial Activity: Finding and using capital optimally
  - Securing necessary funds from banks, investors, or business partners
  - Managing financial resources efficiently to support business operations
4. Security Activity: Protecting property and employed personnel
  - Includes hiring security personnel, constructing security infrastructure, and implementing safety measures
  - Protects both physical assets and human resources
5. Accounting Activity: Stock taking, balance sheets, cost analysis, and record-keeping
  - Tracks inventory levels, financial transactions, and profitability
  - Analyzes revenue, costs, and profit margins
6. Managerial Activity: Overseeing and coordinating all other five activities
  - Provides overall direction and ensures integration of different business functions
  - Applies management functions across all operational domains
Important Note: These activities are interconnected and interdependent, not mutually exclusive. Certain aspects may receive emphasis in multiple categories simultaneously.
Five Functions of Management (POTCC)
Fayol outlined five core functions that management must execute across all business activities:
Planning:
- Charting courses of action for future periods (six months, one year, two years ahead)
- Determining resource needs and acquisition strategies
- Identifying financial requirements for new projects or departments
Organizing:
- Providing material and human resources needed for the structure
- Arranging necessary resources and defining roles
- Establishing the framework for implementation
Commanding:
- Supervising and leading staff
- Guiding employees through policies and procedures
- Defining key performance indicators (KPIs) to measure worker performance
- Establishing HR policies and operational guidelines
Coordinating:
- Bringing different activities and departments into harmony
- Ensuring various business units work together toward common objectives
- Integrating sub-activities within broader functional areas
Controlling:
- Verifying that all activities conform to adopted plans, policies, and regulations
- Monitoring compliance with established standards
- Ensuring operations align with organizational direction
Fourteen Principles of Management
Fayol formulated 14 principles to guide organizational management:
Work Structure and Discipline:
1. Division of Work: Specialization of tasks to increase efficiency and expertise 2. Authority and Responsibility: Managers must have authority to give orders and accountability for
- results
3. Discipline: Employees must obey and respect organizational rules and regulations 4. Unity of Command: Each employee should receive orders from only one supervisor (contrasts with
- Taylor's multi-supervisor approach)
5. Unity of Direction: The organization should follow a single plan of action to guide all workers People and Priorities:
1. Subordination of Individual Interest: Personal goals must align with organizational objectives;
- organizational goals take precedence
2. Remuneration: Workers must be paid fairly for their services
3. Centralization: The degree of centralized decision-making should depend on the specific business
- context
Organizational Structure and Operations:
1. Scalar Chain: A clear hierarchical line of authority should exist from top to bottom 2. Order: There must be a place for everything and everything in its place; systematic organization 3. Equity: Fair and equal treatment of all staff members 4. Stability of Tenure: Low employee turnover is essential for organizational efficiency 5. Initiative: Employees should be encouraged to take action and make decisions to support
- organizational goals
6. Teamwork: Collaboration and unity among team members are essential for achieving organizational
- objectives
Thematic Organization: These principles center around achieving organizational goals and maintaining unity of purpose, emphasizing that individual interests must be subordinate to collective organizational objectives.

Significance in Management Evolution
- Taylor's Contribution: Established the scientific approach to work organization and efficiency through empirical methods
- Fayol's Contribution: Created a comprehensive administrative framework applicable across all organizational functions and levels
Together, these two theorists provided complementary perspectives on management:
- Taylor's work on shop floor efficiency represents the technical/operational dimension (falls under Fayol's technical activity)
- Fayol's framework encompasses all dimensions needed to run a complete organization, from technical operations to financial management to human resources coordination
Conclusion
The evolution of management thought progressed from the division of labor concept to Frederick Taylor's scientific management focused on shop floor efficiency, and subsequently to Henry Fayol's comprehensive administrative theory. Fayol's framework, with its six business activities, five management functions, and fourteen principles, provides a holistic approach to organizational management that encompasses and extends beyond Taylor's specialized focus. His work established fundamental principles still recognized in modern management education and practice, particularly in introductory management courses where these concepts form foundational curriculum components.
