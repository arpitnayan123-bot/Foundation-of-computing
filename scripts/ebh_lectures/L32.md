# Lecture 32: Impact of World War I on Indian Businesses and the Swadeshi Movement

Overview
This lecture examines how World War I (1914-1918) created significant opportunities for Indian businesses and entrepreneurs, leading to the rise of major industrial groups. The lecture also explores the relationship between these emerging Indian businesses and the Swadeshi movement, highlighting a mutual connection between economic nationalism and the Indian independence movement.
Historical Context
- Previous focus: Colonial period (East India Company era) and Indian entrepreneurship from 1700s-1900
- Current focus: World Wars' impact on Indian businesses, particularly World War I
- Future focus: World War II impacts in the subsequent lecture
World War I's Impact on British Manufacturing and Indian Markets
Restructuring of Global Markets
- British manufacturing shifted entirely to war efforts (weapons, military supplies, uniforms, food for soldiers)
- This created a void in catering to domestic demand and colonial markets
- Indian businesses stepped in to fulfill both domestic civilian demand and military requirements
Sharp Decline in British Imports to India
Three major product categories experienced significant import drops:
Cotton and Textile Products:
- Total net cotton imports fell by 50% (3,000 million yards to 1,500 million yards)
- Cotton piece goods declined by 75% (over 250 million yards to 63.3 million yards)
- This massive reduction created opportunities for Indian textile manufacturers
Steel:
- Imports dropped by 84%, falling to approximately one-tenth of pre-war levels
- Pre-war imports: ~1 million tons (1913-1914)
- Post-war imports: ~165,000 tons (1917-1918)
- This shortage created demand for domestic steel production
Other Products:
- Railways, heavy machinery, and other industrial goods also experienced significant import reductions
Indian Businesses and Industrial Growth During World War I
Steel and Heavy Industry: TISCO (Tata Steel)
- Production during WWI (1914-1918): Supplied 1,500 miles of steel rails and 300,000 tons of steel to the British military
- Average annual production: Approximately 60,000-70,000 tons per year
- Government procurement: By 1919, the British government was purchasing 90% of TISCO's steel output
- Impact: Growth and establishment of Jamshedpur as a major steel city
- Significance: Long-term switching costs and technical expertise made TISCO an indispensable supplier
Textile and Clothing Industry
Tata Textile Mills (established in Mumbai/Bombay):
- Benefited from military demand for uniforms, blankets, and other textile supplies
- Established as Empress Mill in Nagpur (1887) and Swadeshi Mill in Mumbai (1887)
- Grew significantly during WWI due to combined military and domestic civilian demand
Sarabhai Group (Calico Mills in Ahmedabad):
- Rose to prominence in the western textile centers alongside Tata
- Later diversified into chemicals, dyes, and pharmaceuticals
- By the 1960s, became a major pharmaceutical company before losing market share to newer competitors
Jute Industry (Eastern India - Bengal)
- Looms growth: Increased from 30,000 to 39,000 over a 10-year period (with further growth likely after 1914)
- Primary use: Packing material for grains and military supplies
- Profit peaks: Reached 7 crore rupees in 1918
- Birla Group:
  - G.D. Birla was a jute trader in Calcutta
  - Family fortune quadrupled during WWI, reaching 4 million dollars (from approximately 1 million dollars pre-war)
  - Became one of India's major industrial groups
Geographic Distribution of Industrial Growth
- Western India (Bombay and Ahmedabad): Cotton and textile industries
- Eastern India (Bengal): Jute industry
- Central India (Jamshedpur): Steel and heavy industry
Connection Between Indian Businesses and the Swadeshi Movement
Nationalist Capitalist Class
- Indian businesses that grew during WWI became nationalist in character and ideology
- Major business leaders like G.D. Birla came into contact with Mahatma Gandhi in 1916
- Business magnates became significant supporters and financiers of the independence movement
Mutual Support System
Business support for Swadeshi: Indian entrepreneurs funded and supported Swadeshi leaders and movements Swadeshi support for business: The Swadeshi movement, which originated in 1905 as a response to Bengal's partition, created demand for indigenous goods This created a symbiotic relationship where:
- Businesses gained capital and reached markets
- The Swadeshi movement gained financial backing and resources
Contemporary Movements
- The Champaran Movement (1917), led by Gandhi, coincided with the WWI period
- Subsequent movements like the Khadi movement and Non-Cooperation Movement (1920s) received substantial support from the nationalist capitalist class
Key Takeaways
- World War I created unprecedented opportunities for Indian industries by eliminating British competition
- Major business groups (Tata, Birla, Sarabhai) that dominate modern India trace their prominence to this period
- The rise of Indian businesses was intrinsically linked to nationalist ideology and support for independence
- The Swadeshi movement gained both ideological and financial strength through its connection with emerging Indian industrialists
- By 1918-1919, Indian businesses had established themselves across multiple sectors: steel, textiles, jute, and railways
