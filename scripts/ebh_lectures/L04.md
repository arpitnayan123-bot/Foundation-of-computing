# Lecture 4: Introduction to Economic and Business History

This is the first live session of the Economic and Business History course delivered by Dr. Kanchan. The course is structured to introduce students to economic systems, business evolution, and historical analysis over approximately 14-16 weeks until the end of November, with alternative weekends designated for doubt-clearing sessions with teaching assistants.
Course Structure and Modules
Overall Organization:
- The course comprises five main modules covering global economic systems and Indian business history
- Modules are supplemented with recorded lectures, live sessions, presentations, and resources available on the Learning Management System (LMS)
- The instructor's background includes PhD research on India's pharmaceutical industry and the role of brands in economic policy
Module One: Introduction to Economic and Business History
- Covers foundational concepts including introduction to economics, business, and history
- Examines how economies affect business, how societies affect business, and how businesses affect both economy and society
- Establishes the foundation for understanding the interconnections between these three domains
Module Two: Early Economic Systems (Pre-15th Century Onwards)
- Discusses agrarian economies and their characteristics
- Explores guild systems as professional associations that controlled both professions and markets
- Examines how agricultural-based economies functioned and their social structures
Module Three: Industrial Revolution and Its Impact (10-12 Sessions)
- Analyzes the agricultural revolution that preceded industrialization in the UK
- Covers the rise of factory systems, steam engines, and technological innovations
- Examines labor movements, working conditions, and the emergence of concepts like the 9-to-5 workday
- Discusses how industrial revolution changed society, labor organization, and economic structure
Module Four: History of Business (19th-20th Century)
- Focuses on evolution of management thought and scientific management principles
- Explores division of labor, assembly line production, and mass production
- Analyzes the rise of large corporations and their development over time
- Covers the 1850-1950s period
Module Five: Indian Business History (Pre-1947 Period)
- Examines the rise of Indian entrepreneurs and businesses like Tata and Birla
- Discusses how businesses survived during the colonial period
- Analyzes the impact of world wars on Indian businesses
- Explores the Swadeshi movement and its significance
Key Conceptual Definitions
Economics
- Defined as the study of production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services
- Encompasses two branches: macroeconomics (country/state level) and microeconomics (individual/firm level)
- Provides the rules and logic governing how societies organize economic activity
- Macro-level focuses on overall economy while micro-level examines individual behavior and incentives
Business
- Defined as an economic activity involving production and sale of goods and services with the motive of earning profit while satisfying human needs
- Serves as a mechanism through which societies organize production, distribution, and consumption
- Distinguished from non-governmental organizations which satisfy needs without profit motive
- Can fail in markets, leading to government interventions or NGO involvement
History
- Not merely about dates and years but about understanding change over time
- Focuses on "how" and "why" events occurred, supported by evidence
- Examines context, time, and change as three critical dimensions
- Involves studying sources such as policy documents, parliamentary debates, annual reports, court verdicts, and historical records
Economic History and Business History Defined
Economic History:
- The study of how and why the rules and logic of economies have changed over time
- Examines governmental policies, economic revolutions, and transformations in economic systems
- Analyzes reasons for policy changes and their implications for society
Business History:
- The study of how and why firms, entrepreneurs, and businesses changed over time
- Examines diversification, sector evolution, and technological changes
- Analyzes why businesses enter or exit sectors and how they adapt to economic changes
Important Themes and Connections
Context, Time, and Change
- Historical changes occur over extended periods, not instantaneously
- Example: LPG policies (Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization) initiated in mid-1980s but fully implemented in 1990-1995 and beyond
- Industrial Revolution took centuries to fully manifest its effects on society
Interconnection of Systems
- Economic systems profoundly affect social structures and organization
- Industrial Revolution changed not only production methods but also family structures, working hours, and leisure time
- Policy changes at macro level have micro-level implications for individuals and businesses
Global and Indian Context
- First three modules focus primarily on Western European and global economic development
- Modules four and five incorporate Indian examples and context
- Indian pharmaceutical sector, despite being post-industrial, shows patterns influenced by early economic principles
Sources for Historical Study
- Policy documents and government records
- Parliamentary debates and proceedings
- Company annual reports and business records
- Court case verdicts and legal documents
- Historical advertisements and media
- Personal accounts and correspondence
Reference Materials and Resources
Students are provided access to:
- Recorded lectures released sequentially as the course progresses
- Presentations uploaded to LMS by support staff
- Recommended textbooks including "Global Economic History: A Very Short Introduction"
- Online resources and public domain materials where possible
- No mandatory textbook purchases; course materials are designed to be comprehensive
Teaching Methodology
- Live sessions combined with recorded lectures provide flexibility
- Teaching assistants (six available) conduct doubt-clearing sessions on alternate weekends
- Interactive sessions encourage student questions and discussion
- Real-world examples from various sectors illustrate theoretical concepts
- Emphasis on understanding "why" and "how" rather than memorizing dates
Key Takeaways
The course establishes that understanding economic and business history requires analyzing not just what happened but understanding the context in which events occurred and the extended timescales over which change happens. By combining the study of economic rules and logic, business evolution, and historical evidence, students gain comprehensive understanding of how modern economies and businesses developed and continue to function.
