# Lecture 7: Overview of Economic and Business History: Interconnections Between Economics, Business, and Society

The course has established frameworks for understanding relationships between economics, business, and society, and now examines the reverse causality: how businesses shape societal structures and economic conditions.
Key Questions Addressed
- Can businesses change or influence society?

- Which parts of society are influenced by business activities?
The Origin and Evolution of the Weekend: A Case Study
When Did Weekends Emerge?
The correct answer is: Industrial Society
Weekends did not exist in agrarian, feudal, or nomadic societies. While these societies had occasional off- days, they were religiously connected rather than work-related. The concept of standardized weekend holidays emerged specifically during and after the Industrial Revolution.
Transformation of Work Organization
Pre-Industrial (Agrarian) Society:
- Work was dependent on daylight hours
- Work duration varied seasonally (7-8 hours in winter, 10-12 hours in summer)
- No standardized working hours existed
Industrial Society Changes:
- Work shifted from open fields into factories
- Artificial lighting (electric bulbs) enabled 24-hour work possibilities
- Standardized working hours became feasible regardless of season
- Work became separated from natural light cycles
Work-Life Balance: A Concept Born from Industrial Change
Definition
Work-life balance refers to the management of time and energy between professional responsibilities and personal life, involving the setting of boundaries between work time and leisure time.
The Labor Movement and Standardized Hours (1880s)
Long Working Hours Problem:
- Initial working day: 10-12 hours
- Initial work week: 6-7 days (72-84 hours per week)
- Resulted in worker exhaustion with no time for personal life, family, or rest
Worker Movement Response:
The labor movement divided a 24-hour day into three equal 8-hour segments:
- 8 hours for work
- 8 hours for rest/sleep
- 8 hours for personal time and family
This conceptualization led to the famous "9 to 5" working hours standard.
Early Adoption and Religious Factors (1908)
- A cotton mill with Jewish workers introduced a five-day work week
- Needed to accommodate Saturday religious observance (Sabbath)
- Workers received both Saturday and Sunday off
Ford's Role in Popularizing the Weekend (1926)
Economic Context:
- Ford Motor Company began shutting down factories on all Saturdays and Sundays
- This occurred during the economic crisis leading to the Great Recession of 1929
Strategic Reasoning:
- Increased leisure time aimed to boost consumer demand
- More free time meant greater consumption possibilities
- Increased sales and economic activity for companies like Ford
Fair Labor Standards Act (1938)
Context: Part of the New Deal during the Great Depression
Key Provisions:
- Standardized 40-hour work week
- Overtime pay requirements
- Minimum wage guarantees
- Ban on child labor (addressing widespread child exploitation during industrial era)
Significance: First legislative protection of workers' rights; recognized weekend and work-life balance as fundamental needs How Businesses Affected Society: Multiple Dimensions Formation of the Working Class
- Industrial revolution created a new social class that did not exist previously
- Led to labor and working class movements seeking workers' rights
- Contemporary parallel: Gig economy workers organizing for rights
Positive Effects of Business on Society
- Employment creation: Jobs for large segments of population
- Innovation: New products and services
- Economic growth: Rising GDP and development
- Tax revenue: Funding for public services
- Skills development: Population becomes expert in certain economic activities
Negative Effects of Business on Society
- Exploitation: Unfair wages and working conditions
- Inequality: Wealth concentration and wage stagnation for working classes
- Pollution and environmental damage: Industrial pollution and resource depletion
- Consumerism: Excessive consumption patterns
- Social disruption: Changes in family structures and social norms
How Businesses Affect Economies
Positive Economic Effects
- Job creation and rising incomes
- Population skill development in specific sectors
- Industry growth and expansion
- Faster overall economic growth
Negative Economic Effects and Crisis Example
The 2007-2008 Financial Crisis:
Root Cause - Risky Lending Practices:
- Banks abandoned traditional credit verification processes
- Issued "NINJA loans" (No Income, No Job, No Assets) to unqualified borrowers
- Prioritized market expansion over sound lending practices
Consequences:
- Massive loan defaults
- Billions of dollars in losses for financial institutions
- Global financial system collapse spreading from developed to developing nations
- Widespread unemployment and economic recession
Interconnection Between Economic and Social Crises
Example: Great Depression (1930s)
- Economic crisis led to reduced birth rates
- Families could not afford to raise children
- Social structure fundamentally altered
- Led to "Boomer Generation" (1945-1960s) as birth rates recovered post-World War II
Key Insight: Business behavior leading to economic crises simultaneously creates social crises, demonstrating how interconnected these systems are.
The Cycle of Business-Economy-Society Influence
Macro-Level Effects
- Technological changes (artificial lighting) enable new business models
- Industrial revolution restructured entire economies and societies
Meso-Level Effects
- Organizational decisions (Ford's factory shutdowns)
- Religious considerations
- Economic circumstances
Policy-Level Interventions
- Initially: Voluntary organizational changes driven by labor movements
- Subsequently: Government legislation protecting workers' rights
