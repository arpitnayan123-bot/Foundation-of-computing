# Lecture 33: Impact of World War II on Indian Businesses

This lecture concludes the course on Economic and Business History, focusing on how World War II affected Indian industries and businesses from 1939 to 1945, building upon the foundation established during World War I.
Overview of Context
- By the start of World War II, India had a strong nationalist capitalist class that emerged during World War I, including prominent business groups like Birla, Tata, Sarabhai, and Godreje
- The Indian independence movement had significantly strengthened, with leaders like Gandhi rising to prominence from 1916-1917
- Indian businesses had already accumulated capital and experience from World War I opportunities
Trade Disruptions
Impact on UK Imports:
- UK imports to India dropped from 31.4% to 21.2% by 1945
- This represented a further decline from reductions already experienced during World War I
Disruptions with Western Partners:
- Trade with France, Belgium, and Netherlands was disrupted after Nazi Germany occupied these countries
- These disruptions affected imports that came from the western side of India
Eastern Trade Disruptions:
- Trade with Burma, Malaya, and Singapore was severely affected after Japan captured these regions in 1941-1942
- This represented a more severe situation compared to World War I, affecting trade on both eastern and western fronts
Growth of Indian Industries
Textile Industry Expansion:
- Dramatic increase in military uniform demand: from 75,000 items per month pre-war to 7 million items per month by September 1941
- This represented nearly 1,000 times growth in demand within 2-3 years
- Number of cotton textile mills increased from 389 to 417 mills
- Mills operated at significantly higher capacity to meet wartime demands
- Cities like Kanpur gained prominence as specialized uniform production centers
Leather Products:
- Military boots and leather equipment output increased from 3 million pounds in 1940 to 14.5 million pounds in 1941
- This represented significant expansion within a single year
Steel and Engineering:
- Tata Engineering and Locomotive Company (TELCO) established a special plant in Jamshedpur to manufacture armored vehicles
- These vehicles were produced in thousands for the British Army fighting in North Africa
Additional Industrial Growth:
- Steel production increased substantially
- Paper, cement, and chemical production received major boosts
- Demand for raw materials and equipment expanded significantly
Differences Between World War I and World War II Impacts
World War I:
- Growth limited mainly to consumer goods (textiles, jute)
- Focus on raw materials and basic steel
- Foundation-building phase for Indian businesses
World War II:
- Broader industrial diversification occurred
- Growth extended to chemicals, armor production, machine engineering, and cement
- Indian firms leveraged already-established foundations to expand into new sectors
- Enabled businesses to clear domestic debts to the UK and establish India as a creditor nation
Consequences for Small Businesses and Society
Supply Chain Issues:
- Severe shortages of raw materials
- Uncertainty in supply chains affecting production and planning
- Small businesses particularly vulnerable due to lack of capital reserves
Impact on Consumers:
- Resource Diversion: Food grains and resources diverted from civilian population to military efforts
- 1943 Bengal Famine: One of the major human tragedies resulting from resource diversion, causing millions of deaths across the Bengal region
- Uncontrolled Inflation: Food prices rose dramatically while wages remained stagnant
- Workers and consumers struggled to afford basic necessities
Capital and Business Consolidation
- Large firms thrived: Companies with substantial capital could survive uncertainties and invest in new production facilities
- Small businesses struggled: Lacked resources to weather supply shortages and inflation
- Competitive advantage: Big business houses like Tatas and Birlas consolidated their market positions
Long-term Impact on Indian Independence Movement
Nationalist Capitalist Class:
- Business leaders became more influential in shaping post-war India's economic vision
- Industrial leaders began planning for independent India's economic future
The Bombay Plan:
- Major business leaders convened in Bombay during World War II
- Created a comprehensive plan recommending government policies for industrial development
- Addressed issues of government control versus private sector operation across different industries
- This plan influenced post-independence economic planning and industrial policy
Course Overview Summary
The course covered five major modules:
- Introduction to Economic and Business History: Defining economics, business, history, and their intersections
- Pre-Industrial Economies: Agrarian systems and guild structures in medieval Europe
- Industrial Revolution (16th-18th centuries): Transformation through steam engines, factories, and technological innovation
- Evolution of Business Management (1850-1950): Division of labor, scientific management, and rise of large corporations
- Indian Business History (1800s-1947): Evolution of Indian industries through colonial period and independence struggle
Conclusion
World Wars I and II fundamentally transformed Indian businesses from colonial enterprises into a significant industrial force. While World War I established the foundations of Indian capitalism through textile and basic steel production, World War II enabled diversification into chemicals, heavy engineering, and defense manufacturing. These developments strengthened nationalist capitalist leaders and positioned them to influence India's post-independence economic policies, though societal costs— particularly the Bengal Famine and inflation—were severe. Subsequent courses will examine how these businesses evolved in post-1947 independent India.
