How Economy Influences Businesses
License Raj in Pre-1991 India
- The Indian government maintained strict control over business operations through licensing systems and industrial policies
- Industrial Policy 1956 created three schedules (A, B, C) that determined which sectors allowed private enterprise
- Government approval was required not only for manufacturing but also for:
  - Production capacity and quantities
  - Pricing of products
  - Distribution methods and channels
Case Study: Bajaj Auto and Two-Wheeler Industry
- Bajaj Auto obtained a license from the Italian company Piaggio to manufacture Vespa scooters
- The collaboration with Piaggio lasted from 1960-1969
- In the 1970s, government policy shifted to promote domestic companies, leading to:
  - Termination of the Piaggio collaboration
  - Bajaj Auto began manufacturing Bajaj 150 and Bajaj Chetak independently
- These products remained dominant in Indian markets until the 2000s
Key Insight: The economy sets the rules of the game—businesses operate within government-defined parameters including capacity limits, pricing controls, and distribution restrictions.
How Society Influences Businesses
Cultural Adaptation of Products
McDonald's entered India without its signature beef-based menu items, recognizing that:
- A significant portion of the Indian population is vegetarian
- Cultural and religious preferences strongly influence food consumption
The company introduced products like the McAloo Tikki Burger to cater to local tastes.
Technology Platform Adaptation
- Netflix produces content in multiple regional languages (Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam) when entering new markets
- Global streaming platforms dub content in local languages to align with consumer preferences
- This demonstrates how companies customize services to match cultural consumption patterns
Advertisement and Promotional Campaigns
- Advertisements reflect societal values and cultural shifts
- Example: Cadbury Dairy Milk campaigns shifted from depicting boys as active sports participants (1990s) to showing girls as athletes (2011-2012), reflecting increased opportunities for women in sports
- Advertisement campaigns are often the first business elements to reflect cultural changes
How Technology Influences Businesses
Technologies Enabling Remote Communication
Digital transformation requires multiple interdependent technologies:
- Internet and high-speed broadband
- Wireless technology (Wi-Fi)
- Computing devices (laptops, computers)
- Peripherals (cameras, microphones, keyboards)
- Software platforms and applications
Netflix Case Study: Business Model Transformation
Netflix's evolution demonstrates how technology fundamentally reshapes business models:
- 1997: Founded as a DVD sales company
- Late 1990s-Early 2000s: Transitioned to DVD rental business
- Mid-2000s: Introduced streaming service as a new revenue stream
- 2012-2013: Streaming became the primary business model
- 2020-2023: Discontinued DVD rental business entirely
Critical Enabling Factor: High-speed internet technology developed in the mid-2000s made video streaming commercially viable.
Technology-Driven Changes:
- New business models emerge from technological capabilities
- Consumer behavior shifts to adopt new consumption patterns
- Older technologies and business models become obsolete
- Companies must adapt their brand identity and market positioning
Example of Technology Making Products Obsolete
- Smartphone cameras made traditional analog cameras obsolete
- Kodak's failure to adapt to digital photography led to bankruptcy
- Companies that do not adopt emerging technologies risk becoming irrelevant
Technology's Impact on Multiple Business Dimensions
- Products: Creation of new offerings, modification of existing products
- Markets: Opening entirely new markets and customer segments
- Production: Automation, efficiency improvements, and backend infrastructure changes
- Distribution: Changes in how products reach consumers (direct-to-consumer, digital platforms)
- Advertising: Targeted, personalized campaigns replacing mass-market approaches
- Content Delivery: Movies released directly on streaming platforms rather than through traditional theatrical distribution
PESTEL Framework for Business Environment Analysis
Understanding business environments requires analyzing:
- Political: Government policies and orientations
- Economic: Fiscal policies, market regulations, economic structures
- Social/Sociocultural: Cultural values, social movements, consumption patterns
- Technological: Technological innovations and infrastructure
- Environmental: Physical environment concerns
- Legal: Laws and regulatory frameworks (often interconnected with political factors)
Bidirectional Relationship: How Businesses Affect Society and Economy Businesses Impact Society Weekend Creation: Workers' movements in the late 19th century demanded reasonable working hours, leading to the establishment of the 40-hour work week and weekend breaks
- This societal change was driven by industrial businesses' practices and worker demands for change
- Government policies subsequently aligned with this shift (standardized office hours, bank closures on weekends)
Businesses Impact Economies
2008 Financial Crisis: Banks issued "NINJA loans" (No Income, No Job, No Assets) to unqualified borrowers
- Risky lending practices in the financial sector triggered a global economic crisis affecting manufacturing and multiple sectors
- Government intervention through bailouts and policy changes helped economies recover
Interconnection of Influencing Factors
- Technology does not act in isolation—it influences and is influenced by economic policies and social adoption
- Consumer behavior changes reflect both technological availability and cultural shifts
- Business decisions must account for economic regulations, social preferences, and technological possibilities
- These factors create feedback loops where changes in one area trigger changes in others
Module One Summary
The five lectures establish that:
- Economics (policies and regulations) shapes how businesses operate
- Society (culture and values) influences what products businesses offer
- Technology enables new business models and market opportunities
- Businesses reciprocally influence economies and society
- All factors are interconnected rather than independent variables
