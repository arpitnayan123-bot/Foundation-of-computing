# Lecture 6: Economic and Business History: Society's Influence on Business Operations

Overview
This lecture explores how society shapes business decisions and operations. While previous lectures examined how economics affects businesses, this session focuses on the critical relationship between societal factors and business practices. Businesses do not operate in a vacuum; they function within specific socio-cultural contexts that directly influence what they produce, how they market products, and whom they serve.
Key Questions Addressed
Question 1: Why Don't All Businesses Sell the Same Products Across Every Country?
Question 2: Why Do Companies Change Their Advertisements, Products, and Services Over Time?
These questions form the foundation for understanding how society influences business strategy and adaptation.
Case Study 1: McDonald's and Localization
The Context
- McDonald's entered India in 1996, following the liberalization, privatization, and globalization (LPG) reforms of 1991
- The company recognized that its standard US menu would not work in the Indian market
The Challenge
- Indian society has significant vegetarian consumption patterns
- A large segment of the population avoids non-vegetarian food on certain days due to cultural and religious practices
- Traditional McDonald's menu items were unsuitable for this market
The Solution: Localization
- McDonald's introduced the Macaloo Tiki Burger in 1998 (just two years after entering India)
- This product combined traditional Indian street food (aloo tiki) with the burger format
- The product became one of McDonald's main offerings in India, not just a side option
- This demonstrates localization: adapting products, production, and services to cater to local audiences, tastes, and preferences
Additional Example: Netflix
- Netflix introduced local content in regional languages (Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam, Gujarati, Marathi, etc.)
- This strategy reflects how companies adapt their offerings based on cultural differences like language and entertainment preferences
Case Study 2: Cadbury Advertisements and Social Change
Comparing Two Advertisements
Mid-1990s Ad:
- A man played the main game
- A woman watched from the sidelines
- When the ball went for a six, the woman came onto the field to celebrate
2021 Ad:
- A woman played the main game
- A man watched from the sidelines
- When the ball went for a six, the man came onto the field to celebrate
The Significance
This reversal reflects major societal changes in gender roles:
- In the 1990s, women's roles were more passive and spectator-oriented
- By 2021, women's participation and agency had expanded significantly
- Opportunities available to different genders had evolved
- Businesses adapted their advertising to reflect these changing social values
Key Insight
Though both ads targeted the same geography and language (Hindi, Indian context), the societal changes necessitated different messaging. Companies must update advertisements to remain relevant to evolving social values.
Components of Society That Affect Business
Culture
- Food habits and preferences (e.g., vegetarian vs. non-vegetarian consumption)
- Language and communication (local vs. global)
- Clothing patterns (evolution of fashion preferences)
- Gender roles (opportunities and societal expectations)
Family Structure
- Joint family systems (prevalent in Indian context, where extended families live together)
- Nuclear families (more common in Western countries and urban India)
- Individualistic vs. collectivist values (affects purchasing decisions and family-oriented marketing)
Additional Societal Factors
- Social norms (acceptable behaviors and practices)
- Demographics (age, population distribution, income levels)
- Education levels (literacy and consumer sophistication)
- Values and traditions (what society deems important)
- Religious practices (festivals, dietary restrictions, celebrations)
- Consumer preferences (what people choose to buy and why)
How Society Influences Business Decisions
Product Development
- Food items designed around dietary preferences and restrictions
- Clothing adapted to cultural norms and climate
- Technology and services localized for regional languages and contexts
Advertising and Marketing
- Advertisements reflect contemporary gender roles and family structures
- Marketing campaigns align with cultural values and traditions
- Messages adapt as societal attitudes evolve
Supply Chain and Distribution
- Product availability adjusted for seasonal festivals and celebrations
- During festivals like Diwali, Rakhi, and Holi, businesses increase inventory of relevant products
- Distribution systems designed to meet regional demand patterns
Employment Practices
- Local hiring requirements based on community expectations
- Workforce composition reflecting demographic realities
Festival-Based Purchasing
- Different products purchased during different occasions
- Businesses must anticipate seasonal demand driven by cultural traditions
- Marketing campaigns timed to coincide with significant cultural events
The Embedded Nature of Business
Core Principle
Businesses are deeply embedded within society. They do not operate independently but function within a socio-cultural context that shapes their every decision.
The Relationship
- As society changes, businesses must adapt
- Socio-cultural contexts influence what businesses produce
- Social evolution directly impacts how companies operate
- Consumer choices reflect societal values and preferences
Broader Framework
Businesses operate at the intersection of multiple influences:
- Economic context (rules, regulations, market conditions)
- Societal context (culture, values, traditions, demographics)
These factors work together to shape business strategy, product development, marketing, and operations.
Conclusion
The lecture demonstrates that businesses cannot succeed by applying a one-size-fits-all approach globally.
Companies must recognize and respond to the unique socio-cultural contexts of different markets and societies. As society evolves—through changes in gender roles, values, family structures, and preferences —businesses must continuously adapt their products, services, and marketing strategies to remain relevant. Understanding society's influence on business is essential for comprehending how modern enterprises operate and succeed in diverse global markets.
