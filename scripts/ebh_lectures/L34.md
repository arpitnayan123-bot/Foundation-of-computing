Module 5: Indian Business History
Colonial Period and East India Company (1600s-1813)
- Rise of East India Company: Established in 1600, the East India Company was granted monopoly trading rights in British colonies
- Powers Granted: The company possessed administrative, military, and judicial powers, and was uniquely authorized to declare war or end wars independently
- Decline of Powers: From 1723 onwards, the company's powers gradually diminished due to widespread corruption among officials
- Administrative Changes: The position of Governor-General of Bengal was established, and courts were created to reduce the company's control
- Trade Monopoly Ends: Although military and judicial powers were removed, trade monopoly persisted until 1813
Role of Indian Entrepreneurs (1600s-1813)
- Indian merchants initially functioned as intermediaries and middlemen for the East India Company
- Evolved into financiers and bankers supporting company operations
- Notable Entrepreneurs:
  - Wadia Group (established 1736): Engaged in shipbuilding and later became prominent in textile and apparel industries through "Bombay Dying" brand
- Played crucial roles in opium and cotton trading networks
Major Commodity Trades (Post-1813)
Opium Trade
- Became the dominant export commodity from Indian shores
- Represented 17% of exports in the 1820s, rising to over 30% by the 1850s
- Part of a triangular trade system: Britain imported tea from China; Britain exported opium from India to China in exchange for tea
Indigo Trade
- Accounted for approximately 25-27% of union exports in 1828-29
- Declined significantly to 6-10% by the 1850s due to market saturation and price crashes
- The crash of 1834 led to multiple business failures, including the collapse of Palmer and Company
Raw Cotton Trade
- Ranged from 15-21% of exports throughout this period
- Essential raw material for British textile mills during the Industrial Revolution
- Cultivated extensively in India to supply Manchester and Lancashire factories
Indian Business Houses in Agency Trade (Post-1813)
Dwarka Nath Tagore
- Established Carr Tagore and Company (1834)
- Founded Bengal Coal Mines (1843)
- Purchased coal mines from competitors, pioneering the coal mining industry in India
- Grandfather of renowned leader Rabindranath Tagore
Sir Jamshetji Jijibhoy
- Established Jamshetji Jijibhoy and Company
- Primary trader in opium from western and central India
- Key supplier and partner to Jardine, Matheson and Company (a major international firm)
- Expanded into cotton trading as well
Emergence of Modern Indian Industries (Post-1850s)
Textile Mills
- Kovasji Nana Bhai Daba: Established Bombay Spinning and Weaving Company (1854), began operations in 1856
- Tata Group: Established multiple mills including Alexander Mills and Empress Mills
- Production surge driven by protective policies after the 1857 rebellion
Other Industrial Ventures
- Tata Iron and Steel: Entered steel manufacturing sector
- Godrej: Initiated business with lock manufacturing (Anchor brand locks, 1857); expanded to soap and steel industries in the 1900s
These companies remained competitive and continued operations into modern times.
Impact of World Wars on Indian Businesses
World War I (1914-1918)
Economic Conditions
- British manufacturing was entirely diverted to war effort
- Dramatic reduction in imports: 75% drop in cotton imports; 50% reduction in net cotton imports
- Steel and railway supplies significantly reduced
- Indian industries captured the market space vacated by British manufacturers
Business Growth
- Tata Steel: Experienced major growth supplying steel rails and war materials
- Tata Textiles: Expanded production to meet military uniform demands
- Birla Group: Rose to prominence in jute production for military supplies
- Three main sectors benefited: steel rail business, textile manufacturing, and jute production
Nationalist Impact
- World War I coincided with major independence movements
- Mahatma Gandhi returned to prominence during this period
- Champaran Movement (1917): Protested exploitative indigo farming practices
- Indian businesses began supporting the independence movement, creating a "nationalist capitalist class"
World War II (1939-1945)
Economic Expansion
- Imports affected not only from Britain but also from France, Belgium, and Netherlands (occupied by Germany)
- Demand for war supplies expanded industrial production across multiple sectors
- Diversification into chemicals, machinery, armaments, and engineering sectors
- Inflation and shortages created challenges for smaller businesses and consumers
Social Impact
- Supplies diverted from civilian to military use
- Bengal Famine (1940s): Partially attributed to deliberate supply diversion and British wartime policies
- Significant hardship for general population despite industrial growth
Rise of Nationalist Capitalist Class
- Indian business houses provided financial support to the independence movement
- Birla House: Notable supporter of Mahatma Gandhi and the freedom struggle
- Businesses recognized that independent India would need economic planning and infrastructure development
The Bombay Plan (1944-45)
- Developed by major Indian business houses in anticipation of independence
- Proposed comprehensive economic policy for independent India
- Many recommendations from this plan were implemented by the post-independence Indian government
- Demonstrated that Indian businesses were not merely profit-seeking but also nation-building oriented
