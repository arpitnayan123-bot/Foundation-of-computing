# Lecture 27: Module 4: Evolution of Management Thought, Rise of Corporations, and Impact of World Wars on Businesses

Overview of the Lecture
This lecture focuses on the significant impact of World War I (1914-1918) and World War II (1939-1945) on global business practices, industries, and economic structures. The discussion examines how wars transformed government-business relationships, sparked industrial innovation, and reshaped the global economic landscape, with particular emphasis on the rise of the United States as an economic and military superpower.
World Wars: Timeline and Key Context
World War I (1914-1918)
- Allied Forces: Great Britain (and British Empire), France, Russia (until 1917), Italy, United States (from 1917)
- Central Powers: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Ottoman Empire, Bulgaria
- Outcome: Allied victory; Treaty of Versailles imposed heavy penalties on Germany
World War II (1939-1945)
- Allied Forces: Great Britain, Soviet Union, United States (from 1941), China, France
- Axis Powers: Nazi Germany, Japan, Italy
- Outcome: Allied victory; Japan's surrender following atomic bombings of Hiroshima (August 6) and Nagasaki (August 9)
Important Context
- Wars were concentrated primarily in Europe and the Pacific
- Many countries, particularly colonies, were drawn into conflicts due to their colonial status rather than independent choice
- India participated in both wars as a British colony
Major Impact 1: Increase in Government Control and Rise of State Capitalism Shift in Government's Economic Role Before the World Wars, governments primarily:
- Regulated through licensing and policies
- Facilitated trade through raw material provision
- Did not control production quantities, prices, or raw material allocation
During the World Wars, governments began to:
- Allocate raw materials
- Control production quantities and prices
- Direct factories toward military production
- Regulate imports and exports comprehensively
Emergence of State Capitalism
Definition: Government controls what to produce, how much to produce, where to sell, and at what price
- This represented a departure from traditional capitalism's free market principles
- Similar to concepts later seen in pre-1991 India's License Raj
- Demonstrated that government-regulated economic development was viable
Purpose
- Military production required massive industrial mobilization
- Industries shifted from consumer goods to military supplies
- Raw materials were allocated strategically for war efforts
Major Impact 2: Growth of War Industries and Innovation
Automobile Industry
Renault (France)
- Transitioned from civilian vehicle production to manufacturing tanks
- Developed the Renault FT tank, recognized as the world's first modern tank
- Design became foundational for modern tank development
Ford Motor Company (United States)
- Shifted from civilian automobile production to military vehicles and aircraft
- Perfected mass production techniques during this period
- Produced tanks, aircraft, and other war-related materials
- Connected to earlier management theories discussed in previous lectures
Chemical and Explosives Industry
DuPont (United States)
- Increased production capacity by 70 times between 1914 and 1917
- Supplied approximately 40% of all explosives to Allied forces by 1917
- Example of how war demand created massive economic growth and employment
- Generated tax revenue for government, stimulating broader economic activity
IG Farben (Germany)
- Synthesized fuel and rubber from domestic coal
- Supported Nazi Germany's war effort during World War II
Consumer Goods Industry
Gillette (United States)
- U.S. government ordered 3.5 million safety razors and 32 million blades in 1917
- Established daily shaving as standard practice for military personnel
- Provided guaranteed government contracts, ensuring revenue and market expansion
Coca-Cola (United States)
- Successfully lobbied government to exempt the company from sugar rationing
- Justified exemption based on boosting troop morale
- Established 64 overseas bottling plants near combat zones (primarily World War II)
- Expanded globally during war and maintained international presence in peacetime
- Notably operated in Nazi Germany under the brand name Fanta
Information Technology and Computing
IBM and Dehomag (German subsidiary)
- IBM's German subsidiary supplied punch card computing machines to Nazi Germany
- These machines were used for population enumeration and administration
- Supported war effort despite IBM being a U.S. company
- Example of businesses operating across war lines for profit
Technological Innovation
- Operations Research: Developed in World War II when resources were limited and needed optimal allocation
- GPS Technology: Originally created for military purposes; later adapted for civilian use (Google Maps, navigation apps)
- General Pattern: Military innovations often transitioned to civilian applications in peacetime
Major Impact 3: Disruption of International Trade and Restructuring of Emerging Markets Impact on Emerging Markets (India)
- Traditional trade routes disrupted during wartime
- British manufacturing capacity diverted to military production
- Supply of finished goods from Britain to colonies severely reduced
- Created opportunities for domestic industries in emerging markets
Rise of Indian Businesses
- Indian businesses stepped into supply gaps for both military and civilian needs
- Industries produced military weapons and supplies
- Sectors involved: steel, rails, jute, textiles for military uniforms
- Captured market share previously held by British manufacturers
- Contributed to the restructuring and development of emerging market businesses
Long-term Effect
- India's participation in World War II (as British colony) created domestic military demand
- Local businesses gained experience and market access
- Foundation laid for post-independence industrial development
Major Impact 4: Geopolitical Shifts and Emergence of U.S. as Superpower Comparative Effects: United States vs. Europe Aspect United States Europe Industrial Production Expanded significantly Severely impacted Aspect United States Europe Infrastructure Remained undamaged Heavily destroyed (factories, railways, cities) Exports Increased substantially Struggled due to damage Economic Position Rose as superpower Weakened Military Status Emerged as military power Diminished Why U.S. Benefited Disproportionately
- Geographic distance from combat zones protected infrastructure
- Supplied war materials to all Allied forces
- Companies expanded capacity and gained experience
- Employment increased; consumer spending rose
- Tax revenue strengthened government finances
- No post-war reconstruction needed domestically
U.S. Emergence as Superpower
World War I: U.S. began rising as economic and military power
World War II: U.S. cemented superpower status through:
- Industrial dominance
- Military might
- Development and use of nuclear weapons (August 1945)
- Positioned as number one global power militarily and economically
Geopolitical Consequences: Decolonization
Post-World War II Developments (After 1945)
- European powers weakened significantly, unable to maintain imperial control
- Power shifted from Europe to United States
- Global consensus emerged against repeated warfare
- Recognition of need to grant independence to colonies
Wave of Independence
- Multiple Asian and African colonies gained independence
- India achieved independence following strong freedom movement
- Many countries gained independence during 1945-1950s period
- Emerging nations began formulating independent economic policies
- Set stage for new international business relationships
Summary of Business Impacts
Four Major Effects:
1. Government Control and State Capitalism: Governments took comprehensive control of production, pricing, and resource allocation, demonstrating viability of government-regulated economic development 2. Growth of War Industries: Massive expansion across automobiles, chemicals, explosives, consumer goods, and technology sectors; companies like Renault, Ford, DuPont, and Coca-Cola achieved unprecedented scale 3. International Trade Disruption and Market Restructuring: Supply gaps created opportunities for emerging market businesses, particularly in India, to capture domestic and military markets 4. Economic Power Shift: United States rose as primary economic and military superpower while European powers weakened, setting stage for post-war global order Looking Ahead Future lectures will examine:

- Evolution of business policies in newly independent nations
- How emerging markets leveraged wartime opportunities for post-independence development
