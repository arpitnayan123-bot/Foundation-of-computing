The Fundamental Concept of Marketing: Anticipation
Case Studies Demonstrating Importance of Anticipation
BlackBerry's Decline
Stock price gradually declined after Apple launched iPhone in 2007
Reached bottom in 2010 and further declined with Android's launch in 2008
Company eventually went bankrupt due to failure to anticipate market shift
Hertz's Collapse
Faced decline after Uber's 2019 launch with $17 billion valuation
Business model relied on owning car fleet, resulting in high capital and maintenance costs
Uber's asset-light model (outsourced drivers and vehicles) proved superior
Company exited global market by 2018 but remains operational in southern India
Blockbuster's Failure
Movie rental company declined as Netflix evolved from DVD rentals to online streaming
Netflix introduced personalized recommendations (2002) and streaming service (2012)
Despite having competitive advantage, Blockbuster CEO dismissed Netflix as non-competitive
Company went bankrupt as OTT model disrupted industry

Pattern of Organizational Decline
Initial gradual performance drop followed by sudden collapse
Organizations that anticipate market changes pivot successfully before sudden decline
Those unable to anticipate face rapid market exit
Definition of Marketing's Core Function
Marketing is the function that enables organizations to anticipate consumer needs and market evolution.
Success requires understanding not only current consumer requirements but also predicting future needs
and demands. Anticipation allows companies to create products consumers will value before competitors
do.
Financial Value of Marketing
Key Financial Metrics
Top Line: Revenue generated through sales; represents total money earned by organization
Bottom Line: Profit calculated as Sales minus Cost
Fundamental Principle: There must be a top line (revenue) for there to be a bottom line (profit)
Practical Example: Cafe Cold Coffee Sales
Price per glass: 120 rupees
Daily sales volume: 80 glasses
Cost per glass: 70 rupees
Total Revenue: 120 × 80 = 9,600 rupees
Total Cost: 70 × 80 = 5,600 rupees
Profit: 9,600 - 5,600 = 4,000 rupees
Marketing ensures steady revenue streams, directly enabling profit generation and organizational financial
success.
Marketing vs. Selling: Key Distinctions
Fundamental Difference
Selling focuses on pushing products to consumers after creation; emphasizes product attributes and
features.
Marketing begins with understanding consumer needs before product creation; customer-centric
approach precedes product development.
Practical Example: The Pen
Sales-Dominant Approach: Highlights pen attributes (writes flawlessly, black color, good grip, improves
exam marks)
Marketing Approach: Asks preliminary questions to understand customer requirements
Do you prefer ballpoint, pilot, or ink pens?
What is your budget?
Do you prefer fine or broad nib?
What color do you prefer?
Understanding customer needs before presenting product ensures better fit and more successful sales
outcomes.

Marketing Definition
Marketing is the process of:
Understanding consumer needs and preferences
Creating value through product or service development
Communicating value to consumers
Delivering value through satisfactory transactions
When value is successfully delivered, customers become advocates, speak positively about the brand, and
generate repeat business and revenue.
Marketing Activities Identification
Examples of Marketing
Product Design: Smaller shampoo sachets for one rupee targets value-conscious consumers
Promotional Pricing: Restaurant offering 20% discount uses promotion element of marketing mix
Consumer Research: Brand studying student preferences for iced coffee represents marketing
research
Notifications: Zomato sending time-targeted humorous notifications about food deals uses digital
marketing to trigger purchase intent
Point-of-Purchase Placement: Keeping chocolates near billing counter uses retail marketing
strategy to increase impulse purchases
Examples of Sales (vs. Marketing)
Salesperson convincing customer to buy a laptop through direct persuasion
Marketing-Sales Relationship
Sales represents the transaction that results from successful marketing. Marketing creates awareness,
builds desire, and communicates value; sales completes the transaction. Both are essential, but marketing
precedes and enables sales.
