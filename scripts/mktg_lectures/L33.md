# Lecture 33: Market Stretch and Product Portfolio Strategy

Strategy
Overview
This lecture explores market stretch strategies, product portfolio analysis, and the distinction between
business, brand, and marketing strategies. The focus is on how organizations strategically expand their
product lines across different market segments while maintaining portfolio health through consistency and
profitability analysis.
Market Stretch Strategies
Two-Way Market Stretch
Definition: An organization simultaneously launches both premium and economical versions of products
within a specific time period
Timeframe: Can range from months to a couple of years, as long as launches occur within the same
strategic period
Real Example - Accor Hotels:
Upward stretch: Pullman, Legend, Novotel (premium hotels)
Downward stretch: IBIS (economical hotels)
Strategy allows the group to capture multiple market segments
L'Oreal Paris Case Study
Premium Segment: Kéels brand
US-based, founded in 1850
Focus on heritage, craftsmanship, and natural composition with zero chemicals
Price point: ₹3,500-4,000 for face cream
Targets value-conscious premium consumers
Mass Market Segment: Garnier products
Price point: ₹300-400 for face cream
Focuses on volume sales
Targets economically conscious consumers
Strategic Advantage: L'Oreal maintains distinct brand identities while covering both premium and mass
markets simultaneously
Product Line Analysis and Consistency
Core Principle of Line Stretching
Line stretching is not merely about adding products; it's about moving the brand's perceived value
boundary
Success depends on consumers accepting the new price-quality tier

Critical Success Factor: Consumer Perception
A line stretch only works if consumers perceive the brand as legitimately capable of operating at the new
price-quality tier
Example - Tata Motors:
Tata Aria launch (2002-2003) at ₹22 lakhs failed initially because:
Previous Tata vehicles ranged from ₹4-7 lakhs
Aria lacked the design, performance, and quality to justify premium pricing
Consumers rejected the new price-quality bracket
Tata Harrier succeeded by:
Investing in design transformation
Upgrading engine technology and aesthetics
Creating legitimate perceived value for the premium price point
Product Line Analysis Framework
Two Key Elements:
1. Sales and Profit Analysis
Identifies high-performing vs. low-performing products
Products with strong sales and profitability are retained
Low-performing products are candidates for discontinuation
2. Market Profile and Image
Assesses brand equity and consumer perception
High market image with low sales may warrant marketing intervention
Low market image with low sales indicates discontinuation candidates
Consistency in Product Portfolios
Definition: How similar or homogeneous products are within a product portfolio
Apple iPhone Example:
Consistency groups by screen size and price range:
4-inch category (₹30,000-60,000): SE (retained), discontinued 5S, 5, 5C
4.7-inch category: Focused on iPhone 8
5.5-5.8-inch category: Concentrated on iPhone 12 mini and 15
Analysis Process:
Group products by consistency criteria (screen size, price)
Evaluate sales, profit, and brand equity within each group
Retain high-performing products, discontinue others
Product Pruning
Definition: Dropping underperforming products from the portfolio to make room for new launches
Strategic Importance: Essential to maintain healthy profitability as the product portfolio grows
Competitive Consideration: Cannot abandon market segments entirely, as competitors will capture that
space and re-entry becomes difficult

Market Competition Example: OnePlus vs. Apple
OnePlus targeted specific screen sizes (5.5-5.8 inches) with concentrated efforts
Apple's products in overlapping segments (X and 12 series) underperformed during OnePlus's market
entry
Demonstrates importance of maintaining presence across all market segments to prevent competitor
dominance
Strategic Framework: Business, Brand, and Marketing Strategy
Business Strategy
Focus: Overall growth, scalability, and profitability
Key Questions: Will we make money in this business?
Scope: Portfolio growth, business unit expansion, cost management, risk assessment, diversification
Perspective: CEO/CFO level, not product-specific
Brand Strategy
Focus: Positioning and identity in consumer minds
Key Questions: Why buy me?
Elements: Perception, feelings, associations, values, and beliefs
Examples: Premium positioning, sustainability focus, innovation-driven, family-oriented brands
Distinction: Positioning here refers to mental placement, not financial positioning
Marketing Strategy
Focus: Customer connection and communication
Key Questions: How do we sell or market ourselves?
Activities: Awareness campaigns, promotional design, pricing decisions, consumer trend analysis,
360-degree campaigns
Scope: Distribution networks, target audience identification, messaging consistency
Product Strategy Positioning
Placement: Sits at the cusp between brand strategy and marketing strategy
Function: Creates products delivering value, which must be communicated and positioned in
consumer minds
Bridge Role: Links the "why buy me" of brand strategy with the "how do we sell" of marketing
strategy
Detal Case Study: Strategy Application
Business Strategy (Money/Profitability)
Volume growth through unit sales
Value growth through revenue generation
Multiple product sizes and refill packs
Investment in distribution networks
Brand Strategy (Positioning)
Core positioning: "Germ-free home"
Key attributes: Kills germs, prevents infection, ensures cleanliness and hygiene

Brand values: Safety, trust, protection, wellness, medical credibility
Emotional connection: Mother's protection, family care
Marketing Strategy (How We Sell)
Target audience: Caregivers, mothers, hygiene-conscious urban consumers
Communication methods: Positive imagery, demonstration-based proof, dermatologist
endorsements
Key messages: Consistency, 100% protection, guaranteed safety
Distribution approach: Massive retail presence, accessible pricing
Critical Consideration: Strategy Alignment
Hypothetical Question: If Detal positioned as luxury skincare, which strategy layer would break first?
Likely Answer: Brand strategy would break first due to incongruence between trust/protection positioning
and luxury pampering
Marketing Challenge: Messaging contradiction between germ protection and luxury would
undermine credibility
Distribution Impact: Luxury positioning requires fewer, premium retail outlets, contradicting current
economies of scale
Trust Factor: While brand trust might endure, the value proposition would fundamentally shift,
potentially damaging long-term positioning
Key Takeaways
Line stretching requires legitimate brand capability at new price-quality tiers
Consistency analysis is essential for portfolio optimization and product pruning decisions
Market presence across segments prevents competitive encroachment
Business, brand, and marketing strategies operate at different levels and must align
Product strategy bridges brand positioning with marketing execution
Strategic decisions require analysis of profitability, market perception, and consumer legitimacy
acceptance
