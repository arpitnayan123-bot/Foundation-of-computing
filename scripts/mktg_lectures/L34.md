# Lecture 34: Understanding Product Narratives and Brand Humanization

Brand Humanization
Overview of the Lecture
This lecture explores the fundamental concept of building compelling narratives and storylines around
products by connecting product attributes with human relationships and characteristics. The session
emphasizes that successful marketing is not just about selling product features, but about humanizing
products to create emotional connections with consumers.
Philosophical Foundation: Epicurus and Two Buckets
The Two Relationship Buckets
Epicurus, a Greek philosopher, identified two distinct types of relationships:
Utilitarian Bucket: Transactional relationships based on give-and-take. Examples include husband-
wife relationships (division of labor), student-teacher relationships (knowledge exchange), and
customer-shopkeeper interactions (money for product). Most human relationships fall into this category.
Love Bucket: Purely relational relationships based on compassion without expectation of return. The
parent-child relationship is the primary example—parents care for children's wellbeing without
expecting anything in return, regardless of the child's achievements or failures.
Application to Marketing
The core insight is that organizations typically place products in the utilitarian bucket (transactional), while
successful brands place products in the love bucket (relational). When consumers perceive a product only
as an object or transaction, they easily switch to competitors. However, when a brand humanizes its
product by connecting it to human traits and relationships, consumer loyalty increases significantly.
Key Concept: The ABC Framework
Three Essential Components
Every successful product narrative requires three elements:
A - Attributes: The specific features or characteristics of the product (e.g., tire grooves, pressure
valve technology, paint scuff-resistance)
B - Benefits: The practical outcomes these attributes provide (e.g., better grip, faster cooking, easier
maintenance)
C - Human Characteristics: The human traits, relationships, or emotions linked to these benefits
(e.g., safety, togetherness, freedom, independence)
Complete Narrative Formula
A strong narrative connects all three components: Product attributes → Benefits → Human emotions/
traits. Missing any component weakens the narrative's effectiveness.

Real-World Examples
Example 1: CEAT Tires
The Challenge: All tire brands appear identical to consumers—similar products, prices, and technical
specifications.
The CEAT Solution:
Attribute: Advanced groove technology providing anti-skid properties
Benefits: Better grip → Better traction → Better stability → Better control
Human Connection: Featured a father and daughter in advertisements, emphasizing protection and
safety
Result: The narrative transforms a product into a relationship-based brand. The image of a father
protecting his daughter while driving in rainy conditions makes consumers associate CEAT with safety
and protection of loved ones.
Example 2: Asian Paints
The Challenge: Multiple paint brands with similar products and benefits.
The Asian Paints Approach:
Attribute: Scuff-free paint technology
Benefits: Fewer marks on walls → Less maintenance → Less worry about appearance
Human Connection: Newly married couples moving into new homes, emphasizing freedom,
independence, and the ability to enjoy their space without worry
Result: Consumers recall Asian Paints when moving homes because of the emotional connection to
independence and freedom, not just paint quality.
Example 3: Pressure Cookers (Prestige)
The Challenge: How to differentiate a commodity product.
The Prestige Approach:
Attribute: Advanced pressure valve technology with uniform steam distribution
Benefits: Food cooks quickly and perfectly → Less time in kitchen → More time with family
Human Connection: Family togetherness, quality time, shared meals, happiness
Result: The narrative transforms cooking from a chore into a means of creating family bonding and
togetherness.
The Humanization Process
Why Humanization Matters
Prevents Customer Churn: The biggest challenge for organizations is customer retention. When
products are perceived only as objects or transactions, customers easily switch to competitors.
Creates Emotional Loyalty: Humanizing products places them in the "love bucket," creating
relational rather than purely transactional connections.
Increases Recall: Consumers remember brands that evoke emotions and human connections more
readily than those focused solely on features.
Differentiates in Crowded Markets: When all products offer similar attributes and prices, emotional
connection becomes the primary differentiator.

Common Mistakes in Narratives
Many marketers focus only on benefits and human characteristics while neglecting the product attribute. A
complete narrative must include:
Clear identification of the product's unique attributes
Explanation of how these attributes create specific benefits
Connection of benefits to human emotions, relationships, or characteristics
Without addressing the attribute component, the narrative lacks credibility and relatability.
Core Marketing Philosophy
Product strategy extends beyond the product itself. It encompasses:
The physical product and its attributes
The tangible benefits these attributes provide
The emotional and relational connections that humanize the brand
The overall experience and connection developed with consumers
Successful brands recognize that consumers want to connect with products on a human level, not merely
acquire objects. The narrative or storyline is the mechanism through which this human connection is
established and maintained.
Practical Application
When analyzing any advertisement or marketing campaign, consider:
What product attribute is being highlighted?
What benefits result from this attribute?
What human traits, relationships, or emotions are being connected?
Does the campaign successfully humanize the product?
This framework applies to virtually any product or service. Every product can be placed in the "love
bucket" when marketers understand and effectively communicate the human dimension of their offerings.
