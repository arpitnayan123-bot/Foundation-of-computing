# Lecture 37: Common Pricing Strategies and Their Execution

Execution
Overview of Pricing Strategy Module
This module covers common pricing strategies adopted by businesses, focusing on how organizations
determine what price to charge, why they charge it, and how they identify the specific price point. A
structured approach following specific steps is essential for developing an effective pricing strategy.
Promotional Pricing and Loss Leader Pricing
Definition and Concept:
Refers to an aggressive pricing strategy where products are priced below cost to stimulate sales of
other profitable goods
Also known as captive pricing or ancillary pricing
Gillette Mark 3 Case Study:
Initially priced at ₹499, creating a high entry barrier for Indian consumers compared to ₹20-40
alternatives
Company reduced price to ₹399, then to ₹99 to lower the entry barrier and attract more consumers
Manufacturing cost was nearly equal to the selling price, resulting in minimal or negative margins on
razors
Strategy leveraged captive pricing by charging significantly higher prices for blades (₹150-200 per
blade)
Consumers, once committed to the razor ecosystem, purchased more expensive blades to maintain
their shaving experience
HP Printer Industry Example:
HP offers over 1,500 printer varieties to ensure consumers choose their brand regardless of budget
Printers launched at high prices (₹18,000 for colored desktop printer) were gradually reduced
Cartridge prices (₹10,000 for black and white, ₹12,000+ for colored) significantly exceed printer cost
Strategy focuses on high ongoing cartridge revenue rather than initial printer sales
Special Event Pricing
Organizations leverage special occasions and festive seasons (e.g., Diwali, New Year) to increase
pricing
Consumer willingness to purchase increases during special events, maximizing customer value
Consumers often buy products they don't need simply because of the festive occasion
Special Customer Pricing
Examples Include:
Easy Day Club membership
Amazon Prime

Zomato Pro
Characteristics:
Makes customers feel valued and special through exclusive offers
Provides priority delivery, expedited shipping, and special discounts
Creates differentiation and increases perceived customer value
Differentiated Pricing and Customer Segment Pricing
Definition:
Pricing varies based on customer segments rather than being uniform across all customers
Indigo Airlines Example:
Offers different prices for various segments: armed forces, students, senior citizens, minors, families,
doctors, and nurses
Creates perceived value and exclusivity for each segment
Provides differentiation from competitors like Air India
Students particularly benefit from segment-specific discounts
Product Form Pricing
Concept:
Same base product or service is offered in different forms with varying inclusions and exclusions
Pricing changes based on the specific bundle or form offered
Microsoft Office 365 Example:
Business Essential: ₹125 per user/month (limited features)
Business: ₹545 per user/month (comprehensive features for businesses)
Premium: ₹660 per user/month (maximum features)
Same underlying product with different bundles to serve heterogeneous market needs
Netflix and Subscription Models:
Different subscription tiers with varying inclusions (audio quality, ad-free options, simultaneous
streams)
Covers diverse consumer needs and budgets
Effective for serving heterogeneous markets
Freemium Pricing
Model:
Base service provided completely free of cost
Premium features available at additional cost
Spotify Example:
Free tier: playlist access, basic features but with ads and limited audio quality
Premium tier: ₹200-300 per year for ad-free experience, superior audio quality, and skip options
Strategy focuses on volume rather than individual value extraction
Converts free users to paying customers through value enhancement

Yield Pricing (Time-Based Pricing)
Philosophy:
Based on scarcity effect and timing of purchase
Commonly used in hospitality and travel sectors
Hotel Booking Example:
Same room at Taj Mahal Mumbai: ₹20,000 per night on December 31st (New Year's Eve)
Same room on December 8-9: ₹7,800 per night
Price difference of approximately ₹12,000 for identical property and inclusions
Key Factors:
Seasonality affects pricing significantly
Booking near travel dates commands higher prices due to urgency
Consumers have limited flexibility and must book despite higher costs
Applicable to flights, hotels, Airbnb, and similar time-sensitive services
Characteristics of Effective Pricing
A good pricing decision must:
Maximize Company Sales - Fundamental to business success; if pricing doesn't generate sales, it's
ineffective
Deliver Superior Value to Customers - Consumers must perceive value at the offered price to make
purchases, which drives sales
Cover Costs - Pricing must enable the business to cover operational and production costs in a timely
manner
Balance the Customer Value Triangle - Integrate three interconnected elements:
Customer perceived value
Competitor pricing
Cost of production/delivery
Interconnected Benefits:
When customers find value, they purchase
Increased purchases improve sales
Higher sales enable cost recovery
This creates value for both business and customers, qualifying as an effective pricing strategy
