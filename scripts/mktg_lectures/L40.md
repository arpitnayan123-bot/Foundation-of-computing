Information Processing and Attention as a Scarce Resource
The Challenge of Information Overload
Indian consumers are exposed to approximately 4,000 to 10,000 advertisements daily, including
notifications, social media ads, billboards, and brand communications
The human brain's processing capacity is limited to 40 bits per second, while consumers are exposed to
11 million bits per second of information
This massive gap between exposure and processing capacity means it is impossible for the brain to
process all incoming information
Attention as a Filter Mechanism
The brain uses attention as a filtering mechanism to determine which information gets processed
Only information that grabs attention occupies mental space in the brain and gets processed further
Information that fails to capture attention is filtered out and not processed
For brands, this means creating attention-grabbing content is the primary objective of any marketing
communication
Mental Availability and Brand Recall
When content grabs attention, it occupies a space in the brain called "mental availability"
Higher mental availability leads to better recall and makes a brand a preferred or favorable choice
Brands with strong mental availability (e.g., Coca-Cola for cola, Bisleri for mineral water) have higher
recall rates
Consumer preference for a brand depends directly on whether it occupies mental availability space in
their mind
The Three C's Concept in Marketing Communication
What Are the Three C's? The three C's that enhance the probability of an advertisement grabbing
attention and creating mental availability are:
Context
The situation or circumstance in which the product is being introduced
Refers to the problem or need that the product addresses
Example: People want clean clothes (from detergent) but also want soft clothes (to avoid skin rashes)
Establishes why the product is relevant to consumers

Concept
The central idea or storyline being communicated
The core message or narrative that connects to the context
Example: Converting rigidity into softness (using Hitler and Gandhi characters in Comfort fabric
conditioner ad)
Represents the creative idea that makes the advertisement memorable
Content
All the elements and materials used in the advertisement
Includes visuals, characters, imagery, text, and any other creative elements
Example: The characters (Hitler/Gandhi), water, the dipping action in the Comfort ad
The tactical execution of the concept
How the Three C's Work Together
These three elements must work cohesively in an advertisement or campaign
When all three C's are present and aligned, advertisements have significantly higher chances of
grabbing attention
Advertisements lacking all three C's components are less likely to be effective
This framework applies to all types of content: static ads, dynamic ads, campaigns, and collaterals
Practical Application Example: Comfort Fabric Conditioner
Context: People need clean clothes but detergents make clothes rough, causing skin irritation
Concept: Someone as rigid as Hitler can become soft (like Gandhi) when exposed to Comfort fabric
conditioner
Content: Visual depiction of Hitler being dipped into water with Comfort, transforming into Gandhi
The alignment of all three elements creates a memorable and attention-grabbing advertisement
Examination Format and Expectations
Examination Structure
All modules covered throughout the course will be included in major examinations
Question format is entirely subjective; no objective questions (MCQs or MSQs) in final exams
Objective questions are used during midterms and continuous assessments
Maximum marks for final examination: 60 marks
Philosophy Behind Question Design
Questions do not ask for direct definitions (e.g., "What is STP?")
The intent is to assess your understanding and thought process, not rote memorization
Marketing has no single "right" answer; focus is on logical thinking and application
Questions encourage students to bring their own opinions and perspectives supported by concepts and
frameworks
Types of Questions Asked
How do organizations create value for customers? (Support with examples)
What is the difference between a consumer and a customer? (Elaborate)
Which adds more value to business: selling or marketing? (Provide example supporting your claim)

"Marketing is like dating" — Agree or disagree? (Explain with example)
STP framework application questions (e.g., developing segmentation, targeting, and positioning
strategies)
Real-world case studies requiring framework application
Answer Quality and Expectations
Writing Approach:
Quality over quantity: writing more does not guarantee higher marks
A concept explained in 10 lines with clarity scores higher than 5 pages without substance
Use of bullet points, diagrams, and illustrations is encouraged
A mix of bullet points and paragraphs is preferred over long paragraphs alone
Visual representations help demonstrate understanding better
Content Requirements:
All answers must be supported by concepts and frameworks
Support findings with real-world examples from brands you interact with
Personal examples from daily life are acceptable; you don't need to replicate examples from class
The focus is on demonstrating your understanding and grip over concepts, not memorizing specific
examples
Normal verbatim writing without framework support is insufficient
Labeling and Organization:
Clearly label each question in your answer sheet
Question sequence does not matter; you can answer in any order
Proper labeling is essential as the responsibility falls on the student
No specific word limit per question; answer length should match concept complexity
Bringing Creativity to Examinations
Students are encouraged to express creativity through illustrations, diagrams, and visual
representations
If you struggle to express ideas in writing, use drawings and then explain them
This approach gives teachers insight into your understanding of the concept
Creative expression is valued alongside analytical thinking
Guidance for Improving Quiz Performance
Strategy for Multiple Choice Questions
Every option in MCQs appears correct but one is the best answer
Develop the skill of elimination: remove obviously wrong options first
This reduces the number of options to evaluate
The best answer requires understanding the underlying logic, not just surface-level correctness
This skill develops over time with practice across multiple quizzes
Continuous Improvement
Performance improves with practice in subsequent quizzes and courses
Do not be discouraged by initial lower scores; it is a learning process

Becoming proficient at MCQs takes time, especially during the transition from secondary to higher
education
