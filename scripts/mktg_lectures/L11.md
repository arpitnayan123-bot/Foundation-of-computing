# Lecture 11: Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning (STP): Fundamentals and Market Strategy

(STP): Fundamentals and Market Strategy
Overview
Segmentation, Targeting, and Positioning (STP) is one of the most fundamental modules in marketing. It
involves dividing a broad market into distinct groups of consumers with similar characteristics, needs, and
behaviors, then developing targeted strategies for each segment.
Understanding Market Heterogeneity
What is Market Heterogeneity?
Consumers differ in meaningful ways that affect their purchase and consumption decisions
A market appears homogeneous from a distance but reveals significant diversity when examined
closely
Example: While 100 Netflix users may seem similar, they have vastly different viewing preferences (K-
dramas, true crime, anime, documentaries, etc.)
Why Market Heterogeneity Matters
Consumers differ in who they are, what they want, why they buy, and how they buy
Different consumer groups value different product attributes
Understanding diversity enables marketers to identify meaningful differences worth addressing
Defining Segmentation
Core Concept: Segmentation is the process of finding meaningful groups within a market whose
members are similar enough to be marketed to differently. It involves dividing the market into distinct
groups of customers (segments).
Key Distinction
Segmentation is not simply dividing consumers into groups
Divisions must be meaningful and usable to create differentiated marketing strategies
The differences identified must be actionable and relevant for marketing purposes
Example - Face Serum Market
Without segmentation, a generic "daily serum for all skin types" may be acceptable to many but relevant
to few. With segmentation, companies create targeted solutions:
Acne control segment
Barrier repair segment
Hydration and glow segment
Pigmentation and marks segment
Each segment requires different product formulations, ingredient focus, and messaging strategies.

Importance of Segmentation
Why Segmentation is Essential
Enables creation of products that address specific segment needs, concerns, and challenges
Allows for relevant and targeted marketing communications
Different segments require different messaging strategies and product benefits
One-size-fits-all approaches fail to satisfy diverse consumer needs adequately
The Problem with No Segmentation
Products become acceptable but not relevant to most consumers
Marketing messages lack resonance with specific audiences
Consumer problems remain incompletely solved
Competitive disadvantage in heterogeneous markets
Bases of Segmentation
Geographic Segmentation
Definition: Dividing consumers based on their geographic location and climate conditions.
Example - Zodio (Tata):
Delhi: Heavy focus on winter jackets and layers due to cold weather (October-December)
Mumbai: Lightweight, monsoon-friendly clothing for rainy season
Bangalore: Casual streetwear for mild, consistent weather year-round
Application Areas:
Climate and weather patterns
Regional consumption preferences (e.g., rice vs. wheat consumption across India)
Geographic-specific marketing campaigns and product availability
Demographic Segmentation
Definition: Dividing consumers based on measurable characteristics such as age, gender, income level,
and education status.
Example - Bajaj Pulsar:
Target audience: Males aged 17-24 (college-going students)
Messaging: "Definitely Male" tagline with masculine, energetic branding
Positioning: Energy and excitement for young male consumers
Result: Clear demographic targeting created strong brand association
Example - L'Oreal Face Cream Strategy: L'Oreal segments the same product category (face cream)
into three age-based variants with distinct benefits:

Key Differences Between Segments
Within-Segment Similarity vs. Between-Segment Differences
Members within a segment share similar characteristics, needs, and desires
Segments are distinctly different from each other
Requires different marketing strategies, messaging, and product positioning for each segment
Example - Netflix Content Strategy
100 students watching Netflix appear homogeneous from distance
Zooming in reveals six distinct content preference segments (K-drama, true crime, anime, sitcoms,
documentaries, scrollers)
Each segment requires different marketing messaging:
K-drama fans: Emotional messaging
True crime fans: Mystery, twists, investigation focus
Sitcom fans: Newer episodes, feel-good, familiar content messaging
Common Questions in Segmentation
What Market Reality Creates the Need for Segmentation?
Market heterogeneity—the fundamental reality that consumers are different from one another
Why is Age-Based Segmentation (e.g., 18-25 years) Not Automatically Useful?
Age alone is insufficient for meaningful segmentation
Within an age group, consumers have vastly different needs, desires, and preferences
Segmentation becomes useful only when combined with other variables (gender, benefits sought,
psychographic profiles)
Example: 18-25 year-old Netflix users have completely different content preferences
Can Two Firms Segment the Same Market Differently?
Yes, absolutely. Different organizations can segment identical markets using different criteria
Segmentation strategy depends on company objectives, resources, and positioning goals
Segmentation Must Be Meaningful and Relevant
Divisions must create actionable differences that support distinct marketing strategies
Differences should justify varying product formulations, pricing, distribution, and promotional
approaches
Strategic Importance
Why Segmentation Matters for Marketers
Enables targeted product development addressing specific needs
Allows creation of relevant marketing communications

Justifies differentiated strategies across segments
Improves marketing efficiency and ROI
Creates competitive advantage through relevance rather than just acceptance
Historical Context
In mass production eras (1980s-1990s), one-size-fits-all products were viable (e.g., Pond's cold cream)
With increasing competition and product variety, segmentation became essential
Modern markets require targeted approaches to succeed
