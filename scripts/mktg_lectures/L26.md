# Lecture 26: Understanding Consumer Behavior: Motivation, Perception, and Brand Humanization

Motivation, Perception, and Brand Humanization
Overview
This lecture explores fundamental concepts in consumer behavior, including motivation, perception, and
the critical role of attitude in shaping purchasing decisions. The discussion emphasizes how organizations
can create emotional connections with consumers by humanizing brands through relational rather than
transactional approaches.
Key Topics
Motivation and Perception
Motivation drives individuals to act and react based on their needs and desires
Perception is how consumers interpret and categorize products, which differs from objective reality
Consumers naturally store information as broad product categories (e.g., "cars," "toothpaste"), but
organizations benefit by understanding how consumers perceive products differently based on their
individual needs
Perceptual Product Categories
Consumers organize products mentally based on functional benefits and personal perception, not just
product type
Example - Toothpaste Market:
Sensodyne = tooth sensitivity relief
Colgate Total = 24/7 oral hygiene protection
Close-Up = fresh breath
Each brand occupies a distinct perceptual category despite all being toothpaste.
Example - Horlicks:
Original product expanded into multiple variants targeting different consumer segments (children,
adults, diabetics)
This segmentation addresses the heterogeneous nature of markets where different consumers have
different needs
Attitude as a Bridge Between Perception and Decision
Attitude is a favorable or unfavorable response toward something, influenced by multiple factors
Bidirectional relationship exists: perception leads to attitude, and attitude influences perception
Attitude formation depends on past experiences and fresh experiences
Decision Function: Attitude = f(Object, Attributes of Object)
The decision is not based solely on the product itself but on the product's attributes as perceived by the
consumer

The Power of Attributes
Organizations have more flexibility in manipulating product attributes than the core product itself
Example - Borges Olive Leaf Infusion Tea:
Competed against green tea by highlighting superior attributes
2x antioxidants compared to green tea
4x vitamin C compared to green tea
Contains oleuropin (helps regulate blood pressure)
Zero caffeine and zero added flavors
These attributes were perception-driven and positioned Borges as superior to competitors
Customer Retention vs. Customer Acquisition
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): High investment required to attract new customers in an
overcrowded market
Customer Retention: More cost-effective—retention costs approximately one-seventh to one-tenth of
acquisition costs
Customer Churn: Major organizational challenge where consumers leave for competitors despite
previous efforts to acquire them
Focus on retention provides better ROI than constant acquisition efforts
Humanizing Brands: Transactional vs. Relational Approach
Philosophical Foundation (Epicurus, 340-270 BC)
Utilitarian/Transactional Bucket: Most human relationships are exchange-based (you give
something, I give something in return)
Examples: employer-employee, buyer-seller, even family relationships (siblings, spouses)
Love Bucket: Only parent-child relationships are truly non-transactional, based on pure affection
without expectation of return
Application in Modern Marketing
Traditional approach: Treat products as mere objects to be exchanged (utilitarian bucket)
Humanized approach: Connect product attributes with human traits and characteristics (love bucket)
By humanizing brands, organizations shift consumer perception from transactional to relational
Case Study - Tire Advertisements
Problem: Why show father-daughter relationships in tire commercials?
Solution: Humanize the tire by connecting it to human emotions
Daughters feel protected and safe with their fathers
This emotional connection (safety, protection) relates to tire quality
A quality tire provides vehicle stability and safety
The tire becomes associated with protecting loved ones, not just a transactional product purchase
Result: Consumers place the brand in the "love bucket" (emotional connection) rather than the
"utilitarian bucket" (functional value), increasing brand loyalty

Key Takeaway
Organizations succeed by understanding that consumers don't purchase products—they purchase
solutions to their needs and emotional satisfaction. By recognizing perceptual product categories,
leveraging product attributes, and humanizing brands through emotional connections, organizations can
move consumer relationships from transactional to relational, significantly improving customer retention
and long-term brand value.
