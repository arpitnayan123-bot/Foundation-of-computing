# Lecture 13: Segmentation and Targeting in Real-World Marketing: The Tupo Case Study

Marketing: The Tupo Case Study
This lecture discusses the practical application of market segmentation and targeting strategies through
the real-world example of Tupo, an innovative personal care brand for growing children.
Overview of Market Segmentation
Market segmentation involves dividing a homogeneous market into heterogeneous groups with similar
characteristics. Traditional personal care markets were divided into only two categories:
Baby products (0-2 years)
Adult and family products (16+ years)
Tupo identified and filled a previously overlooked market gap for children aged 3-15 years who were "too
old for baby products but too young for adult products."
Tupo's Segmentation Strategy
Demographic Segmentation
Age group: 3-15 years (growing children)
Life stage: Early childhood through adolescence
Psychographic Segmentation
Consumer beliefs centered on age-appropriate products
Preference for natural and organic ingredients
Desire for gentle, non-harsh formulations that prevent skin irritation and infections
Target Audience
Tupo's targeting extended beyond just children to include:
Growing children (3-15 years): Increasingly developing their own preferences and desire for
personal products
Parents of these children: Primary purchasing decision-makers who prioritize child safety and well-
being over price considerations
The key insight: "Children are old enough to have preferences, but young enough that parents still control
the wallet and strongly care about their safety."
Product Composition and Strategy
Tupo differentiated itself through ingredient selection based on traditional home remedies:
Turmeric
Hibiscus
Amla
Shikakai
Other age-appropriate natural ingredients

Rather than marketing products as "organic" or "natural," the brand positioned them as remedies
recommended by grandparents (dadi and nani), creating emotional connection and trust.
Pricing and Market Position
Standard market price for 300ML shampoo: ₹150
Tupo's pricing for same volume: ₹450
Premium positioning justified by age-appropriate formulation and ingredient quality
Parents demonstrated willingness to pay premium prices for child health and safety
Key Business Implications
Niche Market Creation: Identified unmet market demand through careful observation of changing
consumer needs
Dual Targeting: Successfully marketed to both children (preference-makers) and parents (purchasers)
Product Expansion: Extended product line to include face wash and makeup products tailored for
growing children
Brand Ambassadorship: Secured partnerships with influential figures like Farah Khan who aligned
with brand values
Consumer Behavior Insights
Modern parenting dynamics have shifted decision-making patterns:
Children now have distinct preferences rather than simply using parental products
Children actively advocate for specific brands (example: daughters requesting Tupo shampoo with
shikakai)
Parents value age-appropriate formulations over cost savings
Real-World Marketing Lessons
The Tupo case demonstrates that successful marketing requires:
Living through the problem to identify genuine market gaps
Understanding evolving consumer preferences and generational differences
Creating authentic value propositions based on genuine needs
Balancing consumer preference with purchasing power dynamics
Charging premium prices when clear value differentiation exists
Other brands employing similar niche segmentation strategies include Cetaphil, Bioderma, Plum, Juicy
Chemistry, and Mama Earth, all of which identified underserved market segments and created specialized
product categories.
