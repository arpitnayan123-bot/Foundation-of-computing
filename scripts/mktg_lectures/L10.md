# Lecture 10: Understanding Existing State, Desired State, and Consumer Decision-Making in Marketing

State, and Consumer Decision-Making in Marketing
Overview of the Session
This lecture integrates two fundamental marketing concepts—the open vs. closed system of the human
mind and the relationship between existing state (ES) and desired state (DS)—to explain how consumer
decision-making works and how marketers influence purchasing behavior.
Key Concepts Discussed
1. Open System vs. Closed System
Closed System (Default): By design, the human mind is naturally closed and resistant to new
information.
Open System: A closed system becomes open when consumers develop curiosity and interest in a
product.
The Relationship: There is a linear relationship between interest in a product and openness to receive
information about it. The more interested you are, the more open you become to learning about that
product.
Marketer's Role: The job of a marketer is to open this closed system through various tactics—
advertisements, celebrity endorsements, packaging, color combinations, pricing, and discounts.
2. Existing State (ES) vs. Desired State (DS)
Existing State: Your current situation or the state you are in right now.
Desired State: The state you want to reach or where marketers want you to go.
When ES = DS: When both states are at the same level, consumers are satisfied and have no need for
a new product. Their mind remains closed because there is no gap to fill.
Creating the Gap: Marketers create a gap between ES and DS by either:
Pulling down the existing state (making consumers feel their current situation is inadequate)
Pulling up the desired state (showing them they deserve something better)
Doing both simultaneously (most effective strategy)
3. Real-World Example: Itchguard Campaign
Before the campaign: Consumers using traditional remedies (powder, oil, cream) had their ES and
DS at the same level—they were satisfied.
Campaign Strategy: Itchguard highlighted the embarrassment caused by itching in public and how
people judge you. This pulled down the existing state and created social awareness.
Result: The gap between ES and DS widened, triggering consumer action to restore balance by
purchasing Itchguard.
4. The Role of Inertia
Inertia Principle: Once a gap is created between ES and DS, consumers feel discomfort and attempt
to restore equilibrium (similar to Newton's laws applied to consumer behavior).
Consumer Response: Consumers will take action—making a purchase—to bring their existing state
back to their desired state and regain comfort.

Marketing Leverage: The wider the gap between ES and DS, the stronger the consumer motivation
to purchase and restore balance.
5. Connection Between Closed System and ES/DS
A closed system converts to an open system precisely when there is a change in ES and DS.
Consumers become curious and open to information when they perceive a gap between where they are
and where they want to be.
This curiosity (open system) is what allows marketing messages to reach and influence consumers.
6. Linking the Value Purchase Chain
Intention to Purchase: This is a proxy variable for actual purchase behavior.
Why It Matters: Actual purchases are difficult to measure and track, but intention to purchase serves
as a reasonable indicator.
Decision vs. Purchase: Decision-making doesn't always result in actual purchase. Consumers may
intend to purchase but may not due to other factors (lack of money, proximity, opportunity).
Marketer's Perspective: Favorable opinions and word-of-mouth recommendations about a brand
(even without actual purchase) indicate successful marketing because they reflect positive intention to
purchase.
Real-World Examples
Free Mobile Applications
Direct Cost: Negligible (app is free to download)
Abstract Cost: High (personal data collection, notifications, advertisements, difficulty in uninstalling)
Closed to Open System: Users open themselves to the app despite low direct cost because they
accept the abstract costs
Gifts (e.g., Pets)
Direct Cost: Zero (you received it as a gift)
Abstract Cost: Very high (feeding, care, eventual disposal)
Consumer Behavior: The gap between ES and DS widens as the responsibility becomes clear
Television Market: Samsung vs. Sony
Samsung Strategy: Operates on volume by targeting masses with products at various price points
(₹17,000 to ₹5,00,000)
Sony Strategy: Focuses on value by positioning as a premium brand for select audiences
ES/DS Application: Samsung effectively pulls down consumers' existing state and raises desired state
across mass markets. Sony relies more on raising the desired state for aspirational consumers.
Clean & Clear Campaign
Strategy: Simultaneously lowers existing state (highlighting acne/blackheads as a problem) and raises
desired state (clean, confident skin)
Effectiveness: Creating a gap on both levels generates stronger consumer desperation to purchase
and restore balance
Ethical Consideration: This approach makes consumers vulnerable by highlighting insecurities
Kohler (Luxury Faucets and Fixtures)
Strategy: Keeps existing state positive but raises desired state significantly higher

Message: "Your current state is great, but you deserve something better"
Target: Affluent consumers who can afford premium products
Impulse Buying
Definition: Unplanned purchases made intuitively without deliberate thinking about need or want.
Trigger Role: Even impulse buying has triggers (advertisements, discounts, limited-time offers) and
involves some unconscious rational assessment.
ES/DS Connection: Impulse purchases may not involve traditional changes in ES and DS but are
driven by immediate situational factors.
Decision-Making Beyond Purchase
Decision Types: Purchase decisions aren't limited to buying. They include:
Avoiding a product
Speaking positively about a product without buying it
Speaking negatively about a product
Building loyalty without repeated purchases
Satisfaction vs. Loyalty
Satisfaction: Being happy with a product you purchased.
Loyalty: Continuing to choose a brand even when competitors are available.
The Difference: A satisfied customer may not be loyal if they have alternatives. Loyalty emerges
when a customer chooses the same brand over competitors repeatedly.
Attribute-Based Consumer Decisions
Every product has multiple attributes (price, design, quality, social status, utility).
Consumers prioritize different attributes based on their pain points and needs.
A product may follow all marketing principles but still fail if it doesn't address the specific pain points
that matter most to a particular consumer segment.
Measuring Purchase Behavior
Actual Purchase: Difficult and expensive to measure directly.
Proxy Variable - Intention to Purchase: Used by most marketers as a reasonable indicator.
Neurometric Tools: Advanced research methods that measure actual brain responses and actual
purchases (used by large organizations like Nielsen, Coca-Cola, and Google).
Ethical Considerations in Marketing
Marketing operates in "shades of gray"—nothing is purely black or white.
Strategies like lowering existing state can be seen as making consumers vulnerable by highlighting
insecurities.
A full ethical examination of marketing practices is essential (to be covered in dedicated ethics
module).
