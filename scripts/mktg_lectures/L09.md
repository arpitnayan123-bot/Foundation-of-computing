# Lecture 9: Understanding Customer Satisfaction and Loyalty: Key Concepts and Financial Impact

Loyalty: Key Concepts and Financial Impact
Overview
This lecture explores the nuances of customer satisfaction and loyalty through practical examples,
emphasizing how contextual factors influence consumer perception and the critical difference between
satisfaction and loyalty in driving business profitability.
Customer Satisfaction: Context Matters
The Food Delivery Example
Two students ordered the same meal from the same restaurant through a food delivery app with identical
delivery conditions:
Estimated delivery time: 30-35 minutes
Actual delivery time: 34 minutes for both orders
Food condition: Warm with intact packaging
Missing items: Cutlery not included for both
Different Perceptions, Same Service
First student (2-star rating): Expected delivery within 25 minutes based on previous fast orders.
Perceived the 34-minute delivery as disappointing and was frustrated by missing cutlery. Likely to switch to
another app.
Second student (5-star rating): Expected delivery to take 45-50 minutes due to heavy rain. Received
order in 34 minutes with warm food and reliable tracking. Highly satisfied and likely to become a loyal
customer.
Key Insight: Satisfaction is not determined by objective performance alone but by how performance
compares to customer expectations and contextual factors.
The Role of Expectations and Context
Customers evaluate performance against an internal standard (anchor) already present in their minds
Context significantly influences expectations:
First student's context: Previous fast deliveries created an expectation of 25-minute delivery
Second student's context: Rainy weather set realistic expectations of 45-50 minutes
The gap between expected service and perceived service determines satisfaction
Same delivery time (34 minutes) resulted in two completely different satisfaction levels
Satisfaction vs. Loyalty: A Critical Distinction
Customer Satisfaction
Transaction-specific evaluation
Based on a single purchase or service interaction
A satisfied customer may not be loyal

Customer Loyalty
Willingness to continue choosing, preferring, and supporting a brand when alternatives are available
Develops across multiple transactions over time
Builds through consistent positive experiences
Important Distinction: A satisfied customer may not be loyal. For example, a customer highly satisfied
with Zomato might switch to Swigy for a 50% discount offer. True loyalty means continuing to choose a
brand despite better alternatives being available.
Financial Implications of Loyalty
Organizations prioritize loyalty programs because loyalty directly impacts profitability:
Repeat Customer Spending Patterns
Repeat customers spend more than first-time customers on average
Repeat customers have higher order values that increase over time
Repeat customers spend more than twice their original expenditure within two years post-purchase
Conversion Rates
Loyal customers have a conversion rate of 60-70%
New customers have a conversion rate of only 5-8%
Conversion rate: The percentage of items in a shopping cart that are actually purchased
Business Profit Impact
Increasing retention rate by just 5% can increase business profit by 20-90%
Acquiring a new customer costs 5 times more than retaining a loyal customer
If acquiring a new customer costs $100, retaining a loyal customer costs approximately $20
The Value Purchase Chain Process
Components of the Process
Expected Service vs. Perceived Service: The gap between what customers expect and what they
perceive determines the value delivered
Value Recognition: If perceived service meets or exceeds expected service, value is recognized
Intention to Purchase: Positive value recognition drives intention to make a purchase
Actual Purchase: Intention leads to actual buying behavior
Satisfaction: Positive actual purchase experience creates satisfied customers
Increased Brand Value: Satisfied customers place higher value on the brand, product, or platform
Loyalty Development: Higher perceived value increases chances of becoming a loyal customer
Increased Purchases: Loyal customers make more frequent and larger purchases
The Reverse Cycle
If the gap between expected and perceived service is negative, value is not recognized
Low value recognition leads to low intention to purchase
Low intention results in low actual purchase
This leads to low satisfaction and minimal loyalty
Low loyalty further reduces actual purchase frequency

Indirect Relationship: There is also an indirect relationship between satisfaction and loyalty—higher
satisfaction increases the likelihood of loyalty development.
Key Takeaways
Satisfaction is transaction-based: Each purchase interaction is evaluated independently
Loyalty develops over time: Multiple positive transactions across time periods create loyalty
Context shapes perception: Expectations and contextual factors are as important as objective
performance metrics
Loyalty drives profitability: Organizations must invest in loyalty programs and customer retention
strategies
The value purchase chain is cyclical: Positive experiences reinforce loyalty, which drives repeat
purchases and business growth
Customer retention is cost-effective: Retaining loyal customers is significantly more economical
than acquiring new customers
